June 16, 2015

The Federal Bureau of Investigation has trained its sights on the St. Louis Cardinals. Yes, the 2011 World Series Champions are being investigated for alleged hacking into the computer systems of the Houston Astros. We are not talking about jumping on to some private message board for fans to chit-chat about the best after game brewski near Minute Maid Park. We  are talking about full on hacking the friggin mother load of proprietary baseball knowledge held by the Houston Astros.

According to the New York Times, FBI and Justice Department investigators are digging into allegations that front-office personnel for the Cardinals did and old fashion hack-a-rooney on the Astros computer networks and snatched up internal files on trades, scouting reports, and proprietary statistics.

Why?! Why would a team with such a strong track record as the Cardinals want to go and hack into the Astros computers? Ask Richard Nixon. No, not really; he's dead. But the Watergate scandal of the mid-70s is a good indication for a motive. You see Nixon had a lock on his re-election. The Democrats had put up some guy that would make Donald "Turnip" a shoe-in for re-election. But Nixon needed to know what the other side was planning. If we remember anything about history, we know how this story ended. Nixon fell into disgrace and resigned just before he was to be impeached. President Gerald "Trippy" Ford pardoned him before the exhaust from Air Force One cleared from the runway at Andrews Air Force Base.

In this instance, the Cards are a lot like Nixon. They are doing well this year with a solid rotation and some good production at the plate. Aside from a breakdown against the Giants last year, they would have been in World Series against the Royals! It makes so little sense to even think about doing something like this. There is too much to lose and nothing to gain by spying on another baseball club.

But the history lesson aside, these are serious charges floating around against the Cardinals as the club engaged in industrial espionage against a competitor. Time will only tell how this story will unfold and what potential consequences may register against the Redbirds of St. Louis. This isn't even just cheating and getting fined by MLB. We are talking about JAIL TIME. Was it really worth it? This is a federal crime across state lines into computer networks. Not even the MLB brought this up, the friggin FBI is jumping on this corpse. What did the Cards think they were some low-rent NSA?
