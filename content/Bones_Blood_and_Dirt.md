---
title: Bones Blood and Dirt
---

The bullet that struck him was not the one that killed him. It it was
the one that snapped his spine and sent him tumbling down rolling over
and over with his limbs now numb and useless.

The bullet that killed Robert Mancha was delivered point blank to the
forehead. Possibly a mercy shot, but very likely delivered in cold
blood.

The sun cooked Mr. Mancha to a dried husk long before we arrived. His
patchy hair still flutters in the wind. Grass has grown up around his
limbs - likely nourished from his decay. Mr. Mancha was missing one
arm - likely dragged off by a coyote, unlikely to have been taken by his
killer. The bullets will tell us more about Mr. Mancha’s killer. The
coyote is of little consequence.

His boots remained on his decomposed and rotten feet. Mr. Mancha wore
coveralls. His name stitched into a patch above a pocket on the left
side of his chest - or what was the remains of his chest.

His head lay pointed north. His feet south. One arm pointing toward El
Paso. The other, well the Coyote will only know.

The nearest road was over two kilometers away through a ravine and some
harsh desert brush. No track marks remained if he came in by a motor
vehicle. Unlikely they would have remained after all this time, but
nonetheless, doubtful he arrived here by truck.

Mr. Mancha was murdered, but first thrown from a helicopter. Slicing his
pant leg open, we find a badly decomposed leg, but more importantly a
fracture of his lower fibula; a hard impact from above 15 feet can
cause. He was pushed from the helicopter because he was not crumpled on
top of himself but rather laid out as if shot mid stride.

Mr. Mancha was pushed from the open door of the helicopter. The
helicopter landed at least 100 yards away. The Killer then exited the
helicopter and approached Mr. Mancha. Unable to run, Mr. Mancha
attempted to limp away, but the break just above his ankle would not
allow.

This is when the bullet to his spine struck Mr. Mancha.

Possibly a luck shot taken from about 40 yards away. Not with a revolver
or pistol, but a high-powered light rifle that breaks down. The caliber
isn’t too strong otherwise the round would have passed through Mr.
Mancha. It was also the same caliber used against his forehead.

Mr. Mancha’s legs lay stretched straight, he might have had the use of
his arms still, the bullet that hit his spine was close to C-6/C-7,
which is a sweet spot for total immobilization. If you get the bullet
above that area of the spine you will likely completely immobilize the
target. This was not the case for Mr. Mancha the bullet in his spine was
closer to the middle of his spine and left his arms useful, thus his
legs were straight from his attempt to drag himself from his killer.

The killer spoke to Mr. Mancha. Why else would his arm have dropped
straight out and long. Had he struggled with his killer the arms would
have been drawn closer in and then would have dropped to his sides or
lumped over himself has he expired.

Mr. Mancha’s pockets were empty save for his wallet with a photo id, a
credit and debit card, and $27 in cash. He was not robbed either before
or after his death.

I know all of this because I killed Mr. Mancha.

Rest assured, Mr. Mancha deserved his death and his decomposition.
