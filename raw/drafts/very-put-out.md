      “Sure,” I replied and got into the drivers seat.
      The two of them climbed into the back seat and covered themselves with
a blanket. To muffle their horrible sounds I started to play a bit of Tool.
Anema was the song; I felt it was appropriate for the setting and the
circumstance.
      Their rocking in the seat just behind my ears make the Jeep tough to
control as the wheels wildly rolled over the broken asphalt like tank
treads.
       I switched the ignition off after pulling into my driveway, “See you
guys on Monday?” I grabbed my bags from the back seat.
                                      *
      A few weeks later Lisa stopped coming to class. Charlie said, she was
acting weird and wanted the two of them to get an apartment together or
something. I wasn’t listening anymore. I wasn’t interested in the tortured
details concerning the burnout of what they started in that hotel room.
      The semester ended, as all semesters do, and I haven’t seen either of
them since. Perhaps I was the reason they didn’t work out. Perhaps they
were. Perhaps there was nothing there to begin with except for the primal
urges of biology and chemical inoculation. Perhaps I wasn’t the only one
(very) put out.
