The Soldiers Walk

      Unconsciousness gave me a strange dream a feeling of dread and terror
leading into the gratification of this dream. I was walking in a desert.
The sand crunched under my feet. No rifle in my hands only a black pistol
in my right hand. My left hand clutched some spot of violence at my left
side, my stomach aching with the crunch of the sand. Every step felt by my
shoulders with the weight of my body ever increasing. My density gaining
strength and pushing me like the hot sun pounding over my head was fueling
this feeling. The sand is grinding into my boots, the grains cutting dully
into my ankles, my toes and my soles. But I can’t stop. I cannot stop to
clean this sand from my feet. The sand always returns to the places you dig
it from. The sand rules the desert I am only trespassing its space. I was
sweating but now I’ve stopped my canteen long since ran dry. The shaking of
my hand clutching the black pistol is uncontrollable with any amount of
muscle I could muster. My throat dry now and my lips chapped and broken. I
saw a paradise not too far ahead, but it keeps moving. Further, it takes me
further into this endless desert. The wind, if ever it blows, is hot and
filled with sand cutting my face and choking my lungs further.
      The numbness set in, first through a sharp snap to my head. My helmet
dropped to the sand. Then the heat, even in the height of the day, stopped
beating down from the heavens. The thirst in my throat ceased and simply
turned to a hollow emptiness supporting the weight of my head. The shaking
of my hand clutching my pistol ceased for some reason my left hand, which
clutched my bloody side, relaxed and dropped to my hip. The numbness
drained into me. The numbness stopped the sand from scathing me. The
numbness stopped the ever-increasing weight from pushing myself, with every
step I took, deeper into the sand. The numbness stopped everything. And
then the numbness itself stopped. The mirage stopped retreating from my
advance. Finally the mirage encased me. The green grass softened the pound
of my boot. The tree shaded my numb scalp. I laid myself under the limbs of
the tree. I finally stopped the endless loop of my feet continually
progressing forward. I started at them motionless relaxed stretched out
before me. The numb static drained into me as my right hand relaxed the
grip of the gun and the left hand followed the right to the gentle pressure
of the earthy grass. My eyes slipped closed and darkness encased me.
