# The Brainwashing Manual Experiment

The cold, wet air of the Baltimore harbor found its way through the basement window of the rowhouse, carrying a smell of brackish water and coal smoke. 

Dr. Vance Aldrich sat in his metal chair, his fingers idly twirling a heavy silver fountain pen. The desk in front of him was bare save for a leather-bound folder and a small, glass syringe filled with a pale, amber fluid—the soporific compound he’d spent the last three years refining.

"You're not listening, Arthur," Aldrich said. His voice was flat, carrying the clinical indifference of an actuary balancing a ledger.

The man strapped to the wooden table in the center of the room groaned, his body twitching against the leather restraints. His skin was the color of gray chalk, sweaty and raw where the straps had rubbed his wrists. 

"I... I can't," Arthur rasped, his breathing a shallow, wet rattle in his chest. "The voices... they don't stop. They're like cymbals in my head."

"That's just the frequency," Aldrich replied, not looking up from his notes. "Your brain is trying to make sense of the void. In a sensory deprivation tank, the mind doesn't find peace; it finds its own static. It's the same static your grandfather tried to bury in the bedrock of Halcyon House in 1973. It's the debt of your bloodline."

Aldrich stood, his boots thumping heavily on the concrete floor. He picked up the syringe.

"The manual is quite clear on this point, Arthur," Aldrich said, his fingers tapping the glass to clear a bubble of air. "To write a new program, we must first erase the old one. We must make you a blank slate. A tabula rasa. It is not a punishment; it is a foreclosure. We are simply reclaiming the assets."

He stepped to the side of the table, his hand reaching out to clasp Arthur's chin. His fingers were cold, smelling of sandalwood and carbolic soap—the chemical handshake of his laboratory.

"Now, close your eyes," Aldrich commanded, his voice dropping into a rhythmic, hypnotic cadence that seemed to bypass Arthur's ears and strike directly at his cerebellum. "Let the numbers descend. Ten. Nine. Eight..."

As the amber fluid entered Arthur's veins, his eyes dilated into total, unfeeling blackness—the same blackness the Witness would see in the eyes of the soldiers at No Gun Ri in 1950. 

The room grew quiet, save for the slow, mechanical hum of the vacuum-tube signal receiver in the corner. 

"Seven," Aldrich whispered, but Arthur was no longer there to hear it. His conscious mind had been safely un-written, leaving only the empty vessel, waiting for the first command of the new master.

Aldrich capped the syringe and returned to his desk, opening his master ledger to a fresh, white page. He dipped his pen in the black ink and began to write.

"Subject 1624-B. First Degree progression complete. The host is compliant. The draft is ready."
