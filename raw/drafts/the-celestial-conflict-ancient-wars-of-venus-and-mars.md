# The Celestial Conflict: Ancient Wars of Venus and Mars

According to the sources, ancient peoples witnessed the "wars" between Venus and Mars as a series of terrifying \*\*celestial battles\*\* that fundamentally altered the order of the solar system and caused massive upheaval on Earth \[1-3\]. These events were recorded not as myths, but as "chronologically disordered history" in the sacred texts, epics, and traditions of cultures worldwide \[4, 5\].

### \*\*The Battle as Described in the \*Iliad\*\*\*
The sources interpret Homer's \*Iliad\* as a literal description of a cosmic struggle between the planets Mars (\*\*Ares\*\*) and Venus (\*\*Pallas Athene\*\*) \[6-10\].
\*   \*\*The Combatants:\*\* Athene is identified as the planet Venus, while Ares is identified as the planet Mars \[6, 11\]. The "passion" or conflict between them was a result of their \*\*conjunctions or near-contacts\*\* in space \[7, 10\].
\*   \*\*Visual Spectacle:\*\* The battle was seen as a "mighty din" where the wide earth rang and the heaven "pealed as with a trumpet" \[12\]. Ares was described as appearing like "black darkness" or a "dark whirlwind" during these encounters \[13, 14\].
\*   \*\*The Result:\*\* Athene (Venus) was generally seen as the victor, eventually being "cut down" from her high elliptical orbit to a nearly circular one, while Ares (Mars) was described as bellowing with the cry of ten thousand warriors after being struck \[15-19\].

### \*\*The Vedic "Terrible Ones" (Maruts)\*\*
In the Hindu \*Vedas\*, the "wars" were witnessed as the movement of \*\*Indra\*\* (Mars) and his host, the \*\*Maruts\*\* \[20, 21\].
\*   \*\*Appearance:\*\* The Maruts were described as "shining like snakes," "brilliant like fires," and "terrible to behold" \[22, 23\]. They were seen as a troop of newborn comets or meteorites that followed Mars after its collision with Venus \[24, 25\].
\*   \*\*Effects on the Sky:\*\* These entities were seen "hurling the stone in the flight" and creating "darkness even by day" \[26, 27\]. Their movement caused the "back of heaven" to shake \[28\].

### \*\*Global Observations of Planetary Conflict\*\*
\*   \*\*China:\*\* Ancient Chinese chronicles record a time when \*\*"two suns" (Venus and Mars) were seen to battle in the sky\*\*, causing the five planets to be agitated and move out of their courses \[29-31\].
\*   \*\*Mexico:\*\* The Aztecs and Toltecs witnessed the conflict through their respective patron deities. In symbolic ceremonies, the priest of \*\*Quetzal-cohuatl\*\* (Venus) would shoot an arrow into an effigy of \*\*Huitzilopochtli\*\* (Mars), representing the electrical discharges exchanged between the two planets \[32, 33\].
\*   \*\*Babylonia:\*\* The planet Mars (Nergal) was called the "Great Sword-god" because, during its close approaches, its atmosphere stretched into a \*\*sword-like shape\*\* \[34, 35\]. It was feared as the "most violent among the gods" who could "move the Earth off its hinges" \[36, 37\].

### \*\*The Experience on Earth\*\*
When ancient peoples witnessed these "wars," they also experienced their physical consequences:
\*   \*\*Atmospheric Din:\*\* The approaches were accompanied by a "terrible noise" or "agonising crash" from a cloudless sky, likely caused by \*\*interplanetary electrical discharges\*\* \[38-40\].
\*   \*\*Global Instability:\*\* Witnesses felt the earth "quake and tremble" and saw the sun "go down at noon" or "return ten degrees" on the sundial as the earth's rotation was perturbed by the passing planets \[39, 41, 42\].
\*   \*\*Celestial Pageantry:\*\* The atmosphere was filled with "pillars of smoke," "burning pitch," and "shooting flames" as the earth passed through the trailing gases and debris of the combatants \[43, 44\].

Ultimately, the sources argue that these "wars" ended around \*\*March 23, -687\*\*, when the current order of the solar system was established and Mars and Venus settled into their modern, stable orbits \[45-47\].
