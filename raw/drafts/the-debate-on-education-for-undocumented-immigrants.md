Mr. Gonzalez

      Your commentary in the Star-Telegram on Sunday, January 12, 2003
titled “More education for the undocumented? Yes” was appalling to me. I am
a college student and I understand the drive to become something better
than what you were before, but I refuse to ignore the fact that these
“undocumented immigrants” a.k.a. illegal immigrants are breaking the very
foundry of the country that they are illegally occupying. I understand the
need for higher education and the enlightenment that it provides a person
for the rest of their life. I understand that this individual that you
profiled in your article is a hard working individual that is so
desperately working towards his dreams, but he is still breaking the law.
Our borders are what defines us as a nation not just our “American Dream”.
We as a nation are all hard working people because the people that were
here before us worked hard and followed the rules to make this country
great. I believe the Texas legislature was conned into believing that these
“undocumented immigrants” would one day receive their citizen ship with the
stroke of a presidential pen and then there would be nothing but re-
elections for everyone involved. I know how the legislature works and whom
it benefits.


      I have studied American politics every year that I have had to pay my
own way through college. I have not had the abundance of financial aid nor
have I had the blessing of a benefactor like Carlos. Does this make my
dream any less precious only because I am a legal citizen? Are my goals not
worth the trouble to my elected state government for them to protect my
right as a legal citizen? Does my vote not mean anything when it’s counted
against the lobbying power of LULAC? Am I any less of a citizen in this
country just because I am a natural born citizen?  I realize and respect
the sovereignty of my country. For you sir, to debase the heritage that my
country has instilled in its youth is disgusting to me. Can’t you see that
you are demoralizing the very culture that you are benefiting from by
advocating this fleecing of American colleges and universities?


      “Texas legislators caught on quicker than federales that an educated
populace, whether legal or illegal citizen, would bolster both the American
and Mexican economies.” I believe this statement undermines the very
foundation of right and wrong that I have based my entire life on. I
realize that I could sell drugs to pay for my way through college but I
would rather earn legitimate money with a “legal” profession.


      To urge our congressional representatives to grant “the right to learn
the meaning of the American Dream” has no credence in my mind because as an
illegal immigrant/student Carlos and the thousands like him have no rights
under the constitution and bill of rights that I have lived my life by.




                                       William Coleman Dowden III
