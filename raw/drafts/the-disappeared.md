The disappeared.

“I heard them bring you in last night,” a lone voice spoken softly over the
cold almost dead silence. “What have they charged you with?”

      I mumbled my response because they had cut out my tongue.

      “Oh, I see you must have been a writer. A journalist perhaps,” my eyes
focused to the darkness and I saw that he was chained to the wall like me
with only our hands holding our respective body weights aloft. He had a
dirty rag wrapped over his eyes. There were two spots where his eyes would
have been, the spots were stained a deep filthy red.

      “You want to know why I’m here with you?” his question soft with an
underlying urge to simply tell his story.

      All I could do was grunt my approval to his request.

      “ I was a teacher, a physics teacher at the university. When they came
for me I was reading one of Aristotle’s works. Poetics is a beautiful read.
Complicated, but beautiful to read. I was happy to have that be the last
book my eyes saw,” the analogous nature of his story began to unfold and
like mine it seemed built upon the same foundation.

      “I think they wanted me to work in their bomb factories. They took me
to a place far outside the city, in a truck. I feared they were going to
shoot me for something I had said previous to the revolution,” his words
however terrible the story he told I knew was true because of my own
experiences.

      “When I told them I would build them bombs or give them any of my
research, they beat me. When asked again, they took my eyes and took all my
papers and books. I would dare to say that they even took my copy of
Aristotle’s poetics. They brought me here soon after that. They chained me
to this wall and then there was you.”
