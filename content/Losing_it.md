Losing it.

The grinding roar of the atmosphere flashing past the aluminum skin of
the airplane bound them together; a family of strangers. Dads were
flying the plane. Moms were tossing packets of peanuts at the children
safety buckled into facades of safety - a nylon safetybelt. One hundred
and twenty seven people aboard the Boeing 737 in route to San Juan
Puerto Rico. It wouldn't land.

The man sitting in the emergency exit row 14, seat f, had been fighting
a tiresome headache for three days. He hoped it would fade away before
his vacation began, but there he was trying to sleep it off with
headphones on and a Faith No More album playing on his iPhone.

People that remember vinyl remember the skip that would occur when the
record hit a scratch. The sound in his ears was something like that, but
impossible in the digital age. The music had never felt the air it had
always been in the machine. Why would that sound have triggered right
then.

His eyes fluttered open. His headache, gone. But so was everything else
about the man seated in row 14, seat f. His mind flashed to the
stewardess’ instructions and FAA mandated question from before the
flight took off.

“In the vent of an emergency. Will you be able to assist in the
evacuation of the plane through the emergency exit? I need a Yes or No
answer.”

He answered yes, but suddenly his fit arm reached up followed his eyes
as they stared at the red handle with the word “pull” printed on it.

His left hand released his seatbelt. His wife passively read a magazine.
She also had her earphones in. Probably listening to some gardening
podcast. He suddenly remembered their wedding day. At the courthouse in
Washington, DC over 10 years ago. Then a long summer day from earlier
this year. He came home from work to find her in the garden, tending to
her tomatoes. He opened a bottle of wine which was all they had for
dinner that evening and then love making for dessert until they fell
asleep.

He and his wife had no children so they were afforded such lavish
luxuries as wine for dinner.

All that seemed so far away from him right now. Right now, he felt his
sole purpose on earth was to pull the red handle his eyes were locked on
at that moment. It was pulsing in his mind it's the beat of his
headache.

The skip in the song woke him up. Why had the file skipped? Why did he
now want to pull the handle and push outward?

The compulsion was undeniable.

Do it.

He had never heard that voice before. It was labored and lustful;
between his ears.

His right arm reached up and pulled the handle.

The rush of air sucked the door out into the atmosphere and he with it.
His thoughts emptied from his mind blow away by the rush of air
pummeling him. His wife. His mind focused only on her as he saw the
water rush toward him.

The flight never landed in San Juan, Puerto Rico.
