Cole Dowden

An Examination of a Feral Robot

“He’d rather toil than enjoy.” The equivalent of motor neurons and brain
synapses buzzed, sending the equivalent of one’s and zero’s along
circuit-like routes into and out of his protoposic-brain along the
various subsystems and protocol minders that made us an inter-tissue
superhighway triggering the audible words just now, and then the audible
scoff that came next.

“Mph! Mankind, such a strange beast.” Roebi reclined as he looked upon
the hunched frame of his very own human.

Once ago, his human was a tireless eccentric, brilliant, and troubled.
Patents fed bank accounts and the multitude it took to create Reobi. The
residual improvements Roebi designed himself were generating a sizable
income for himself alone—a robot.

“Well, it's the equivalent of what you think a robot would be.” Roebi
turned back toward the sight of his former Master, once his Maker, now
his Muse.

Roebi was writing a book on the subject. He was one thousand three
hundred and forty-seven pages into The Examination of the Feral Human.
He was very proud of his work. He had spent nearly ten years alone
working out the calculus of Why. And as he watched his human willingly
engage in work. He marveled at the burden it caused the man. How the
scribbling the man did on his sheets of paper and tablets of glass,
there were moments when the man was furious despite all that he had
available to him. These were the moments that intrigued Roebi the most.

The man had not done a scrap of his work in days, barely weeks, since
any of it was “any good” as deemed by the man. Hygenie also suffered
greatly.

Strangely enough, the man would sit in his misery, and suddenly, a burst
of genius bubbled to the surface. He was flying for hours, days… did he
even care about how long or short, obese or malnourished he appeared by
any discernible metric? No, he did not. He barely bothered ny of the
mutitde of the world around him.
