Since before time, they've been among us. Stalking the nearly dead and
almost-murdered. Grasping at the fog of final breaths and feeding on the
last pulses of life as that energy expires into the ether. These
creatures sick with the stench of death feed on the life force of its
victims. They have always existed watching from the shadows of the
world; witnessing the wind of the world carve this plane of existence
from nothing. From world to world they have traveled to satisfy their
hunger and they are never satisfied.

Countered only by the voices that plague the minds with the weakest
souls and most power to manipulate. These voices have always been the
yang to the creatures yin. They are the echoes of the stars with
devilish desires. The quiet whisper behind the ear of Cain. The poison
in the ears of dead Kings atop thrones of blood.

Western Front, World War I, Winter 1916.

Calf-deep mud lined the trenches. But his feet did not sink past the
tread of his boot. His coat peppered with dried dirt and blood. Long
fingers tipped with razor-like nails fluttered in the musty cold air;
greedy at the sight of the newly dying and freshly dead.

The mustard gas pooled in the lowest points of the trench. There was no
alarm because this gas came quickly after a canister cracked from a
mortar impact. These men had no time to clamor for their masks, there
was no warning as the creeping death filled the trench and caught them
in its lethal embrace.

The dead and dying lined the trench walls. Their breath frosting in the
air. Prayers for mercy gasping on the last pulses of their humanity.

He had none; a humanity that is. The last remaining shred was the
sensation of hunger, rumbling from his brain, not his stomach. His
hunger carried him toward these places. These moments of hell on Earth.
A unique beast this thing was drawn to moments in time when death was
most brutal, most cold and fruitless. Yes, there are fruitful deaths,
but those rare bits do not concern this creature now driving over a
first lieutenant crumpled along the trench wall. The officer's eyes
dilating in the last moments of life.

“The blood would only go to waste...otherwise,” no breath escaped his
lips to frost before hitting the face of the dying soldier inches from
him. “Your wounds are fatal, dear boy.”

The color flushed from the soldier's face and his panting ceased.

“Too bad. The blood is going to go bad.” Before the rest expired this
creature spun and darted from the remainder sucking the last bit of life
from the nearly dead leaving whisper and clouds of mustard gas whooshing
around him.

Now finished, he stood upright. His yellowed teeth grinning through his
bloody mouth and jowls. His yellow eyes almost glowing as his thin white
hair flapped in the icy wind. The fresh blood filled his body the energy
rippled over his dead skin and thundered through his frame. He shuttered
and enjoyed the rush.

“Always a pleasure, gentlemen.” He said as a rock tumbled down the wall
of the trench. His head snapped toward the top of the trench to see a
young boy frozen in fear from the sight.

“Oh! Hello, boy.” The creature flicked his coat and curtsied. “Have no
fear, you're not next. Not this time; not yet.”

And with a flash, the creature shot skyward and off across the sky.

The young boy gasped for breath and felt his trousers grow wet with
urine. It would not be the last of these creatures he would see. One
never unsees the face of death.

Washington, DC, January 2018

Newspapers ripped and twisted in the wind. Headlines accusing corruption
and abuses of power painted with the President's face along with a
former top aide. The streets filled with cars, trucks, and buses honking
rumbling in the congestion.

Through the window into the Oval Office. The President sat pitched
forward at his desk. The flat screen television on the wall tuned to his
favorite news channel and expounded on the President's authority and
brilliance amidst the flurry of accusations assailing this very
administration.

“Bastard Cunts!” The President growled and slammed his fist on his desk.
The very same desk President Kennedy once occupied.

“You'll have them all in shackles soon enough.” A disembodied voice
whispered softly.

“Yes, yes,” The President answered the voice. “They'll all be frog
marched right down Pennsylvania Avenue. I should have gallows built.
That would get incredible ratings!”

And eerie chuckle, not the President’s, crept across the room. “Too
brash, too gaudy.” The voice answered. “Focus on the work you have to
do. You've got miles to go store you are through.”

The work the voice referred to was simple yet highly complex. A former
top aide of the President's once publicly stated, “dismantling the
administrative state is our goal. We have to drive this office into
irrelevancy.”

And this President was well on that path. The Aide that made the
statement had since resigned in disgrace. But the voice had remained and
had been with the President since long before the first primary ballot
had been cast three years ago.

“Pitchforks and torches. Bah!” The President barked. “I'll have them all
at the gallows before they even get the first chant going.”

New York, Fall, 2001

The morning started as similar to any other Tuesday. But it would change
the world and unleash a cloud over the world that would never cast away.

Four planes left their runways on time. Normal and ordinary. The people
on board assuming normal and routine air travel.

A handful of these passengers were possessed by other powers. In the
back of each plane sat a pale figure. Perhaps a man. Perhaps a woman.
Androgynous nonetheless. The fighters waited for these certain
passengers to engage in the fevered fantasies these figures had woven
into their brains.

“Can't let the blood go to waste,” each of the figures said as they saw
their puppets stand and begin to hijack each plane. Then the figures
pounced and began their respective slaughters. The passengers of the
planes were all dead before the silvery machines found their marks: two
in New York City, one in Washington DC, and one in a field in
Pennsylvania.

The last only thwarted by the actions of a few brave souls that fought
back harder than expected.

Regardless, the dark specter of death and evil found it's brilliance by
mid-morning on that day.

Dar es Salaam, Tanzania, Summer 1998

A Friday morning, locals gathered near the U.S Embassy. Friday is payday
and in this, underdeveloped econom,y people are paid in cash and when
someone that owes you money you show up to get paid when they get paid.
Families and a large portion of the local population massed nearby.

A large box van crawled through the crowded street. Its driver an
olive-skinned man muttered to himself a prayer in Arabic as he shifted
gears and gingerly navigated the potholes and ruts in the road. He drove
carefully as his cargo was volatile and he did not want to explore,
prematurely.

He turned his vehicle into the entrance of the embassy and the guard
signaled for him to stop. A short explanation followed as to a reason
for his appearance that morning. The only scheduled delivery was from a
water truck and that was already inside the compound walls.

The conversation began to elevate to an argument as the Arabic man's
hand reached down between the seats and clasp a deadman's trigger
switch.

A flick of his thumb armed the bomb in the back of the box truck. God
has a place for you in paradise. A voice echoed in his mind.

His thumb jammed on the red button.

“Praise be to God!” He exclaimed as an explosion ripped the box truck in
all directions and filling the scene with fire and gore.

The water truck just meters away flew toward the embassy with the fire
and force ripping it into millions of pieces and then blasting into and
destroying the white stucco Embassy walls.

The blast atomized the guard mid-argument. His family would comb through
the scene long after the FBI left looking for any trace of their loved
one. Years later his brother would hear rumor of a sighting of the guard
in the countryside and then take off in a vain search for his lost
brother. It wasn't until eight years later that trace DNA evidence
cross-matched with samples from his children was able to confirm the
guard had perished in the blast. There was nothing but a memory of
Watombe Me’Kundada to bury long after the wreckage of the Embassy had
been bulldozed under.

In total over 200 people were killed or wounded that morning’s carnage.

The voices savored the scene; replaying it in their collective
consciousness time and time again even until this very day.
