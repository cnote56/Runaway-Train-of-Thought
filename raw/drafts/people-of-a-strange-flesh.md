People of a Strange Flesh

By Cole Dowden

To kill something, you have to know it. Study it. Know where it sleeps, eats, shits, and fucks. You can’t be caught surprised by it’s built-in and leftover randomness - leftover from its former humanity.

Pounding at the door, I have never seen this creature before but I know it. I know how it breathes, the blood in it's veins, and the triggers in it's mind. I know because I killed these things. Because I was good at killing these things. I now know it for other reasons which I’ll get into shortly.

###

A long time ago, I used to be good at knowing these things. I had the best record in the Corps about knowing where they congregate and how and when they moved. I was better than the prediction programs the brass used.

I was good until I wasn’t. Then the second change happened; changing everything. Our defenses went shithouse. Their patterns changed, within moments across the battlefields.

Bullets still worked, but they were smarter still. All at once. This led me to understand my theory on a hive-mind connection was true. If just one caught a glimpse of a map or saw a display screen of tactics then all was lost. Instantly, the frontline of the battle or operation were sitting ducks. I saw it first hand. It felt like they were watching through our eyes and knew our fears and weaknesses.

But that was a long time ago and before my current sitrep.

Turnabout is fair play. It will likely have killed me in the end. Its bite and claw marks now oozing on my forearms. These will be the instruments of my demise, but I've got a few good hours left in me before their toll is due.

Again, the pounding at the door. It used to be human. But then the change occured.

The change didn't happen overnight. It wasn't an election result. The first one was a slow and painful process. Like puberty, the change took hold and we didn't understand it. It just occurred and was irrevocable.

Theories abound, like so many frightened rabbits scurrying out if the underbrush.

Some shouted that vaccines poisoned the youth.

God was to blame.

Beg God for forgiveness.

Plastic in the food.

Pollution in the water.

All the theory in the world did not have much to say when the changes took the one you once knew and made them bare their teeth and come at you. They don't move like “people.” They may still look like a “person.” But they don’t move like one after the change takes hold.

Something switches off the things in our brains that keep us from moving and twisting our bodies in ways and manners that we’d normally recoil from to avoid pain or self-injury. I think their brains just require less and shut off that part of the brain...or maybe it is eaten up by the baser parts. Honestly, I haven’t had time to look deeper as the fastest way to put them down is a shot to the head; so getting a brain to dissect is more difficult than you’d think.

They fight like hell and there’s something in their spit. it burns when it hits human skin. Like a snake's venom, it starts digesting and necrotizing the tissue and flesh as soon as it hits.

I remember seeing it first hand a long time ago, but now I’m feeling it. my forearm. it is the classic defense wound. right across the forearm on the underside. I’ve see guys get this wound in the field. Well, not this one but it always hardens and gets this sticky black shell across the wound. They always try to cover it up. They roll down their sleeve. They distract any attention away from it. I now know why.

The pain fires through your veins and nerves like lighting across the sky and then the thunder shakes the cockles of your heart. Then the little tremors start. Involuntary and uncontrolled, these little earthquakes find parts of your body that you never felt before and awaken something long asleep. Then you feel the change. Some describe it as a temperature change with each of your organs a different temperature.

That is how I got myself here. Our friend on the other side of this door surprised me. I’m rusty. And it’s going to cost me my life. I know this is certain because my wounds have gone hard, black, and sticky.

###

I patrolled the perimeter every morning, noon, and alternating schedules at night - this was my religion. Nothing had triggered on my sensors. I presumed it was a normal day, then I saw the wild boar on the heat sensors. I’d seen it a month ago but didn’t dare go beyond the wire. My rations were still well stocked then. Now, pork was sounding good and I needed the fat to survive the winter. I didn’t debate it in my mind. I shouldered my rifle and checked my K-Bar knife. Clean and good to go.  I headed toward the wire. I wore my chest protector but left the rest of my body armor - legs bracers and arm sleeves - back in the compound. My chest protector did have one thing I could not live without; a brightstar, or that is what we called it. It was a visual and sonic defensive weapon. Once charged and activated it emitted a focused sonic and light barrage of energy directly toward whatever was attacking you form the front. The resulting stun bought you enough time to either shoot or flee - which ever made the most tactical sense.

A few taps on my wrist screen and the sensors were deactivated for me to slip through the barbs and razor wire.

I checked the GPS for the coordinates of the heat signature; 300 yards northwest of the wire. The boar had wandered into a bulge of the river that carved a semicircle of marsh. It was boggy, but I could slog through it to get a meal. I was not too proud to get a little dirty. The boar hadn’t moved in the past hour, I hoped it was stuck in the mud and then would be easy to shoot and then strap to heavy drone-lifter to get it back to the compound.

I approached the blob on my wristscreen. I cut my breathing to short bursts and tried to slow my heart rate. I needed to be ready if this thing charged at me. My hand gripped the rifle and felt the safety lever built-in the the handle click; it was now ready to fire. I carefully stepped through the tall grass; dividing with the rifle barrel.

Snorting and rustling a few feet away.

The musky smell of an animal invade my nose.

But it was not like a hogs or a deer's scent. It was deeper and more wild.

I pressed forward into the grass.

Suddenly, the grass pushed toward me and a roar into my ears. The barrel of my rifle pushed aside and toward the sky ad burst of rounds fired from my fingers pinching against the trigger.

This was not a boar or wild hog. It was man-like and attacking me. It wrestled me to the ground the two of us rolling with the tall grass and into the mud. Gnashing its teeth and punching and clawing at me. I tried to block its thrashing with my forearms lined up a protecting my face and chest. That’s when I felt its bites and slashes.

I should have worn my body armor. I had the whole set left over from my patrol days. It was by the door back in the house. Worthless to me now. I could feel the poison begin to burn into my body from its bites and slashes.

I threw a hard left cross to give me a little space to twist the brightstar device to charge it. THen I saw the face of the thing attacking me - it looked almost human but for the gnashing teeth, green spit, and pale white skin. I threw a right cross connecting again with the creatures jaw. With my left hand I tapped the trigger panel for the brightstar.

Suddenly a high pitched whine and bright flashes emitted between the two of us. Then the bolts of light and sound concussions began. I dropped my arms wide to each side of me to give the creature the full force of the brightstar.

It withstood a few bursts of light and sound, but I felt it recoil away. I rolled and scrambled to me feet. I tapped the brightstar on my chest to release it from my armor and tossed it toward the shirking creature.

My rifle lost in my the tall grass and my K-Bar must have slipped from its scabbard during the fight. I retreated toward the wire and back to the compound. I needed a rifle, I needed something stronger than my fists to fight this creature.

My legs burned as I high-knee’d through the tall grass and mud. I could feel my heartbeat in my throat. The poison from my wounds was starting to seep into my blood system. My vision was starting to blur and show tracers from the blood vessels in the backs of my eyeballs. I took a deep breath and pounded my chest; the adrenaline was my friend right now. I had to get distance between the creature and myself.

I dove through the wire tumbling into the cut grass of the compound. I tapped my locator sensor to activate the wires defenses. My backup rifle was inside the house. I had to get there before the creature barreled through or jumped the wire.

Then I heard it roar.

The brightstar had run its course.

The creature was now on my six. I looked back to see it jump above the tall grass; bounding toward the wire between us.

The damn thing must have been incredibly strong to jump out of the ankle deep mud of that bog.

I was almost ot the porch when the wires electric fencing triggered. Alarms rang out. The auto-guns would be targeting anything that was moving near the wire. Gunshots rang out.

I was at the front door. My bloody hand pressed against the scanner panel. A green light flashed the electronic lock disengaged and I pressed the door open. Spinning on my heels to slam it shut; I saw the creature inside the wire and charging toward me. The gunfire was missing it; like it knew the angles and patterns to slip through the fiery-hail of bullets.

I slammed the door shut only to feel it buck back against me with the thumb and pounding form the creature on the other side of the inch thick steel. I dropped to my knees and pressed my shoulder against the door, praying to hear the electronic lock engage.

Buzzzzzzzzzzz.

It engaged and the creature hammered and roared at the steel between us.

I crumbled and rolled laying out in front of the door. Catching my breath and then the searing pain of my wounds roared against my senses.

I rolled over pulling my knees under me and toward my chest. My arms were in such pain that I couldn’t use them to stand up. I whipped my left leg out and stamped my muddy boot on the hard steel floor. A splatter of mud shot out form the impact in all directions. I shifted my weight and brought my right leg up and lifted my body to a standing position.

Medic kit. I needed the serums and painkillers or I was going to succumb to the venom in my wounds before that creature pounded his way through the steel door.

I looked back to see the fram shaking from its thundering blows.

Damn that thing is strong. I thought to myself.

I got to the medic kit and torn it open the syringes filled with rainbows of serums and painkillers. Like a gay pride flag but complete with bandages and medical tape.

I tore a purple open from the cascade of colors and ripped the syringe cover off with my teeth and stabbed it into my thigh. The rush of the serum burned into my muscle and subcutaneous fat.

I took a yellow one and repeated the prep with my teeth tearing ht ecover from the needle and injected my forearm with the anti-venom treatment - using half for one arm and the rest for the other.

I grabbed the green syringe and flicked the needle cover off; I un-clipped my chestplate pulling it to one side and stabbed my gut with the serum mixture that I thought was a mix of amphetamines and adrenaline. I then saw the red serum marked “WARNING AMP AND AD.” Then I remembered my field medical training. The green was a sedative.

I crumpled to the ground. Unconscious in seconds.

###

I didn't plan for it, but playing possum worked.

I woke up hours later. My wounds throbbing with pain and puss but the hammering at the door ceased. It was quiet. The auto-guns silent. No alarms either.

I thought the creature either was in a bloody heap or he had somehow deactivated them. Or the guns ran out of ammo.

The puss oozing from  my forearms was a good sign. It meant my immune system was fighting whatever that thing had infected me with. It also meant the booster shots I gave myself - purple and yellow - had done what they needed to do. But they didn't clean it all out, which told me this thing was like the others and what ever turned him might still turn me.

I don't have time to waste.

I pulled myself to my feet and looked over the command center monitors for signs of the creature outside. No bloody heap. He was still outside.

The auto-guns were empty. But still tracking movement. But their accuracy was limited in the darkness.

Night fall. The things were more nocturnal than otherwise. He'd be moving more now.

I linked the guns sensors to my wristscreen. At least they'd give me eyes above. I then retrieved my body armor. I'd need all of it.

I suited up with my second assault rifle, ammo, and my second favorite K-Bar.

A quick gulp of a protein drink in the cantina. It was intended to provide only what a soldier needed in the field. It wasn't designed for flavor. And it showed.

My wounds ached, but I didn’t have time to waste on them. The compound was fortified but that creature outside will find a way inside sooner or later. I could not explain it, but I felt it was there staring a the building and waiting for a sign of weakness to attack.

I triggered a drone to liftoff and recon. I followed by watching their on-board cameras via the heads up display in my helmut.

The drone circled the compound. I couldn’t see where the damn thing was outside. I saw the hole in the wire and the marks on the turf leading to the door I entered earlier. Nothing showing where the thing was now.

The auto-guns were still tracking, but it was just because I hadn’t turned them off  - mindless scanning looking for a target big enough to shoot. Reloading them would be a good idea, I thought to myself. I dispatched a maintenance drone to review each one to see if there was anything further needed on them. Regardless if they didn’t even hit the creature, they gave me enough of a distraction last time to be useful at least once more.

AMmo is in the armory. I’ve got five auto-guns. The ammo-reload weighs forty pounds each. I thought to myself. I’ll have to do it one at time I can’t run with two reloads taking both hands, that thing is gonna be watching.

Then I triggered a heavy-lift drone to take-off. I’d get the ammo out of the armory and take it to the roof. The heavy-lifter can then take up at least four of the reloads I needed ands drop them at each auto-gun. I can then run behind and reload.

Good plan. I thought to myself.

THen I tried to lift a forty pound reload with my forearms still wounded and fighting an infection. I had almost no grip and pull of the weight was almost scream worthy.

I grimaced and sucked at my teeth as the pain rippled through my body. It was unlike any other I had felt before. I remember getting stabbed in a bar fight, a lifetime ago. Didn’t hurt anything like this. Lost a tooth in basic training - nothing like this. Dislocated my shoulder  playing football when I was a teenager - not even on the radar with these wounds. Even with the painkillers  the searing pain growled in my veins.

I’d have to push the cart with the ammo to the freight elevator.

Just put your shoulder into it. I talked to myself more these days; more than I’d like to admit.

Thankfully a cart in the armory had six auto-gun reloads already stacked and ready - just had to push it to the freight elevator that led to the roof.

###

I was covered in sweat as I leaned against the wall fo the elevator. The piston underneath drove the platform upwards. Before these damn wounds, I wouldn’t have broken a sweat.

What the fuck was in that damn things spit?

I pulled a stim-shot form my armors first-aid kit, hoping a little stimulant would help me get the reloads the final few feet to the landing pad then the heavy lifters could do the rest.

The elevator opened to rain splattering the rooftop. I shouldered the cart out of the elevator. Then the musty scent struck my nose - and I swear I could almost picture the damned things sight of me pushing the cart out of the elevator. It was right above me on the elevator infrastructure. My damn rifle was leaning against the wall of the elevator. I was slipping because I was tired and sick. The rifle should have been strapped across my chest for just this reason.

The cart was just over the threshold and out of the elevator. I could punch the emergency button three feet to my right and the elevator would slam shut and descend.

I couldn’t shake the mental image in my mind of seeing the cart just outside the elevator and my shadows being cast from the elevator light on the rooftop. It was like I was seeing through the damned things eyes.

Then my mental picture shifted. Like I just tilted my head.

“Fuck,” I said aloud. I was seeing through its damn eyes.

This is what they did when they swarmed us at the bases and in the field. They infected one or a few of us and then watched through our own damn eyes. I saw through his eyes and he saw through mine! We spied on ourselves!

I lunged toward the emergency button and then I saw the mental image swing into action. It swooped into the elevator as the doors slid shut.

Towering above me, I finally got a clear look at the creature.

It’s skin was darkened and splattered with mud, now running from the rain and cleaning it a bit. Its muscles stretched and bulged over its broad frame. Long fingers with sharp black nails tipped its powerful arms. I half torn off button-down shirt remained covering its muscle-caked torso.

Boots. It was wearing boots, but its talons form its feet had punctured the toe boxes - like the foot had grown through the boot.

The half-torn pants looked to be military issue. It worn an ammo belt. The buckle born the insignia of the Corps. This thing was once a soldier like me.

My blood ran cold as I lay exposed to my realization and this creature above me.

Brightstar.

My thoughts betrayed me as I saw the creatures head tilt. Maybe it still did not know what that word meant. My right hand rested over the device on my chestplate.

The elevator began its descent.

Was the thing thinking about the brightstar like I was thinking about it. Did they understand the language I was thinking in?

I tapped the brightstar to engage it. The whine of the charge out grew the elevator groan.

The creature shrieked and threw its arms out wide ready to swipe and shred me to death.

I was wearing my body armor now, but I doubted it would do much good, if this thing knew my thoughts then it also knew the weak points in my armor.

Then my mind focused on a pain I felt in my right upper thigh. I felt like I had been shot. But I hadn’t been shot; I looked at the creatures leg. It’s right pant leg had been torn open and a black viscous liquid seeped form the tear.

Was I feeling its wounds? Were we connected?

Without thinking, i swung my left leg around to land squarely on the creatures wound. Landing my blow with a commanding hit.

Then the pain rolled through the both of us. It crumpled to the floor of the elevator. I shouted and grabbed my right thigh, not understanding the pain I was feeling but suffered no wound there.

Without thinking, I reached for my rifle and the thing did the same. I kicked it again and reached the rifle first.

I felt and saw the creature strike at my body armor; sparks flew. The damn things nails must have been harder than steel! I fired a quick burst of gunfire upwards peppering the ceiling of the elevator. The damned thing slipped the angle of the gunfire; it must have seen where I was aiming the rifle.

It darted from corner to corner of the elevator. The doors began to open; I couldn’t let it get inside.

As I squeezed the trigger it lept toward the elevator’s hatch in the ceiling. I rolled out of the elevator and struck close button with the butt of my rifle. The doors began to narrow and seal.

It was now inside the damned walls.

The elevator shaft had vents and conduits that would let the creature find its way anywhere in the compound. It knew, because I knew. I tried not to think. I tried to just react. Thinking that might buy me a few seconds of time to out maneuver the creature.

I rolled to my knees and charged toward the armory. My arms didn’t hurt as much as before. I felt better actually. Like I had just broken a fever. I heard the creature finding its way into the venting system.

Then the creature burst through the vents in front of me. My wet boots slipped and I slid to its feet. Wires and venting hung from the hole in the ceiling. It was covered in wet dust and growled as it found itself looming over me.

I tapped the brightstar on my chestplate. The burst of light and sound almost deafened me. The creature shrieked and charged over me running in the direction I had just come from.

Blinking my eyes back into focus, I clamored to my feet and started running again toward the armory - in the opposite direction from the creature.

I barreled through the steel door as I heard and saw in my mind the creature charging down the hall toward the armory.

I slammed the steel door shut and pressed my body against it trying to get the electronic lock to seal faster.

Pounding at the door, I saw through the creatures eyes in my minds eye. I could feel the shrieking rip through my vocal cords. I could feel the scraping against the steel on my fingertips and nails.

The electronic lock sealed the door.

The pounding continued as my eyes watched the depiction in my mind. I was still - almost frozen in awe - as I felt the struggle of the creature on the other side of the door.

I touched my fingertips with my thumbs and felt something hard and sharp. Looking down, I saw long thick black nails jutting from my now creature colored finger beds.

Suddenly, I felt stronger and energized.

The pounding from the other side of the door stopped.

My skin had turned the sickly shade I saw on the creatures just moments ago on the roof.

Thousands of memories and feelings flooded my mind. I felt all of them. My heart filled with pride and hate, then sorrow and love. Like my mind was being downloaded and downloading all at once. I was there in the armory in my compound, but i was also on the other side of the door and then miles away lurking in a tree as rain tapped on my brow. I was also at my old command and control room in the city; I saw the monitors flickering illuminating my now dead comrades. Then I was back to the other side of the door.

This now brings me to where I began and now where my old self ends.

###

When it was normal, before I was what I am, I remembered life as a human was so much more complex than it is now.

One day my credit card didn’t work. I cursed my shitty job and the mountain of debt I had clouding my name.

The news reports talked about riots and fires downtown. I scoffed at the fires and violence raging in the streets. Blaming the damned immigrants and criminals from my barricaded home.

Just like a few hours ago and now the same back then the world had changed right under our feet. I had changed before I knew what I was.

I was no longer what I once was. I was now better.

I was now one of many.

E Pluribus Unum.

Out of many, one.

One people.

One of the People of a Strange Flesh.
