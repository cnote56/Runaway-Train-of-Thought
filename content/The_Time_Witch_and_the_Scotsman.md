The Time Witch and the Scotsman

Time traveling dead hooker from the gold rush finds her spirit tethered
to a modern-day detective facing down the corrupt modern world along
with his personal demons.

Why have these two unlikely people become connected?

Where will this connection take them?

What will happen to the Detective and the Time Witch?

Who are they hunting for these answers?

Episode One: The Case of the Red-Eyed Jailer

The red eyes of the beast in prison cell 14D with prisoner 221654
glowed. It was almost twilight and the sun crept toward the horizon
gnawing like the hunger in the belly of the owner of the red glowing
eyes.

A low growl reverberated across the hard concrete room.

Tommy Francs wet himself. Still clutching the hinge of the steel door
and staring into the blackness all around the room. He felt the growl
more than he heard it.

Tommy’s hot and fevered breath jetted from his mouth and nostrils. He
had never felt fear like this before. Not since before when he first
heard a story from his grandfather of how a young boy was lynched for a
whistle cast at a white woman.

The growling vibration shook Tommy form his memory and crawled up
through his senses setting off an uncontrollable shaking in Tommy’s
tendons and joints.

A shard of light glowed at the center of the room between Tommy and the
red eyes.

“Lights out in ten, Inmates!” A gruff shout from a guard at the end of
the cellblock. “If anyone wants to let us know anything that they feel
might improve their sleeping arrangements this evening...Now is the
time!”

The urge to say and do anything to get out of the cell surged in Tommy
Francs’ veins. His hands swiped and meekly tried to knock at the hard
steel door. His voice paralyzed with fear.

It is not for trying. Tommy Francs had been choked with fear from the
moment the guards threw his heaped body into the cell after the brawl in
the mess hall. His last words to the guards were a string of laughs and
coarsely and horrifically crafted slurs meant to inspire additional
physical deterrence.

It was moments after the cell door slammed shut and Tommy finished his
blue streak that the red eyes welcomed Tommy Francs to his cell.

Tommy had not dared utter a sound when the red eyes came into focus from
across the blackness.

He could not have spoken to his mother if he wanted nothing else. Tommy
Francs was in one of 14 cells in a solitary confinement cell block in a
prison found on one of the most isolated places in the known worlds.

“Eight minutes!” A guards voice shouted from the end of the cell block.

In Tommy’s mind visions of blackened hands grasping at him. Long oily
black fingernails claw at his skin. The sharpness of the nails pushing
and finding bite into his orange prison clothes.

“Five minutes!” A guards voice shouted from the end of the cell block.

The hot breath of the red eyes cast over Tommy’s nostrils and mouth. The
sweet decaying and sour smell of the red eyes breath invaded and
polluted the dank cell air.

“Two-minute warning!” A guards voice shouted from the end of the cell
block.

Cold. Intensely cold. Tommy was freezing. But he did not feel the room
get colder. Just him. Tired and growing colder, he felt his energy
dropping as even the uncontrollable shuttering of his body was
exhausting him.

“One minute!” A guards voice shouted from the end of the cell block.

With a light unseen by anything else, Tommy’s heart burst in his chest
as the red eyes glow intensified and pulled Tommy's frame across the
prison cell. The spotlight cast over his body tracing up from his leg,
torso, and chest. Then his arms frozen in mid-air hands and fingers
curled in defensive horror. His skin ashen and white; unusual because
Tommy Francs was in fact American with a dark complexion.

Tommy Francs was dead before lights out.

The growling vibrations in the cell block rolled and transmuted into a
playful humming, like a child playing in its mind while eating its food.

“Time’s up,” The Guard sucked his teeth in disappointment as he turned
the electronic key-lock. Every external door in the solitary confinement
block sealed. One by one the lights flickered and switched off as
blackness took over the scene.

###

A team of three prison guards escorted Tommy Francs’ dead body as it
rolled out of the solitary confinement cell block on a gurney and under
a white sheet early the next morning.

As the procession passed by, “Dead. Just dead.” A flabbergasted Corporal
smacked his clipboard with the back of his right hand. “Cause of death?
Dead. You can’t just die from being dead! Did that overnight guard even
stay here all fucking night?”

Behind him, a tall slender man in black suit with grey pinstripes
stepped into the cell block from the access hallway of the prision.

“I don't need this shit right now. It’s only Sunday!” The guard
continued his exasperation. “Why couldn’t that piece of shit die
tomorrow, when I’m off this damn block for a week!”

“Prisoners die all the time, Corporal.” The Warden interrupted with an
icy tone, “Many alone...in their cells...at night.”

“Sir, apologies, Sir!” The Corporal snapped to attention as the Warden
crossed the hallway sniffing the air after the three guards pushed the
body past.

“No need, Corporal.” The Warden’s slender arms and hands stretched
behind him and clasped together, restrained and safely clutched behind
his back.

Walking past the Corporal and through the steel gates of the cell block,
the Warden stepped into the narrow hallways with seven steel doors on
each side.

Counting the doors with 14D the only one open the Warden cast his head
back toward the Corproal.

“And how many prisons, Corporal, were in this block last night?” He
already knew the answer before the Corporal counted on his clipboard and
cast the answer back toward the the Warden.

The Warden was staring at the cell block. His body resolute and sharp
against the hardness of the walls. The Warden knew the solitary
confinement block had only one other occupant last night.

“Why are these prisoners kept in adjacent cells?” The Warden’s words
sharp and biting. “This is a solitary confinement block, correct? These
prisoners are habitually problematic when kept in close proximity to
other prisoners. Thus the expense of the steel and concrete, correct?”

“Yes, Sir!” Responded the Corporal, almost involuntary form the years of
training.

“Then?” The Warden shot a damming stare back at the Corporal. “Why is
there an inmate in 7D? These inmates are still too needful. Even here.
They will want to talk and scuttlebutt. Regulations state clearly
prisoners must be kept in opposite corners of solitary. For their own
protection.”

“Yes, Sir!” The Corporal responded.

Through the peephole in the door, the Warden peered.

Inside 7D the panting breath of broken man caught the attention of the
Wardens senses. The smell of urine and vomit wafted through the
peephole.

“Corporal!” The Warden shouted. “This inmate has had enough reflection
to assure us of his compliance. He is to go back into gen-pop on the
next rotation, at your discretion.”

Back inside the cell. Omar Rivera clutched at himself still feeling the
echoing pains of the previous nights fear and coldness.

“R...rr...red.” Omar muttered softly. “I...I...eyes.”

The warden spun in his heels and swiftly proceeded back to the head of
the cell-block.

“What about 14D?” The Corporal wanted to ask should he have it washed
out, but a vague utterance was all he could muster as the Warden rushed
by him.

“Prisoners die all the time, Corporal.” The Warden reminded the
Corporal, “Many alone...in their cells...at night.”
