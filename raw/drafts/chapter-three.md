# Chapter Three: The Root and the Ghost

The harbor is a graveyard of rusted industrial relics, the perfect setting for a BIC cleanup job. I see his car parked under a single, flickering streetlamp.

I keep the lead-lined sleeve tucked under my arm, the ledger inside acting as a silent, invisible anchor. My phone—which I’ve set to max volume—is in my other hand. As I approach the driver-side window, the interference from the ledger’s onion-transmitter starts to bleed through the phone’s speaker, a low-frequency, rhythmic grinding sound.

As I get within three feet, the interference hits his car's electronics. The dashboard lights flicker and die. His earpiece emits a high-pitched, piercing whine that makes him physically recoil, clutching the side of his head. He looks at me, and his face goes pale as he realizes the sound is coming from me—or rather, from the bag I'm carrying.

I lean down, resting my arms on the frame of the window. I pull my phone, which is now outputting a steady, guttural white noise. I hold it right near his dash. His onboard computer starts cycling through errors, the screens flashing red: SYSTEM FAILURE. CRITICAL DATA LOSS.

"You said this ledger was a key," I whisper, my tone suddenly dropping all the feigned ignorance. "But it feels more like a bomb. You want to talk about the Sun-Belt? Or do you want to tell me exactly which node in the BIC is going to lose their head when they realize you’ve turned this entire harbor into a dead zone?"

***

I stand in the center of the rotunda. I place the briefcase on the central dais and snap the latches. The sound—a sharp, mechanical click-clack—is like a gavel.

"Gentlemen," I say, my voice steady, echoing off the high, curved ceiling. "I’m Dooling. And I believe we have a debt to reconcile."

I yank the power-core of the holographic projection. The room descends into a hellscape of flashing alarms, screaming metal, and blinding, blue-white fire. 

*Fourth down. Three yards to go.*

The memory slams into me: locker-room heat, the smell of stale sweat, the metallic tang of blood from Coach Henderson’s clipboard strike. "You’re a machine, Dooling! You don't stop until you hear the snap!"

I lower my shoulder, briefcase clutched to my chest, and I run. I hit the laminate wall at full tilt. The wall gives way in a shower of splinters and reinforced mesh. I tumble through into the freezing darkness of a maintenance tunnel, the briefcase still clutched to my chest.

***

I try to stand, but my shadow doesn't follow. I am no longer in a maintenance tunnel. I am jacked in.

The "Web" isn't a series of tubes—it’s a blinding, infinite scaffolding of shimmering obsidian columns that stretch into a forever-horizon of data-amber. I see them: the BIC avatars, twenty-foot-tall constructs of polished gold and shifting static.

"You're curious, Dooling," the Auditor’s voice vibrates through the very air. "The Marshal key is a dead letter. If you trigger it, you don't kill me. You kill the flight controls."

I look up. The Havana sky is gone. I see the tallest, most jagged tower in the distance, surrounded by a storm of swirling, lethal white noise. 

*“He’s not in a tower, Dooling. He’s the foundation. Every trade made, every panic triggered... it’s all archived in his memory banks.”*

A group of "Watchers"—geometric, multi-faceted sentinels that guard the BIC’s towers—begin to rotate, their sensors locking onto my avatar. They know I’m here.

"What do I do?" I ask, my voice sounding like a thousand simultaneous transmissions.

*“Run,”* the Auditor whispers. *“But don’t run away from the towers. Run through them.”*
