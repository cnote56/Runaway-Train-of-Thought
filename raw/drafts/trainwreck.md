Train wreck.


The gentle rocking of the train awoke Simon.  The sun in the autumn sky burned its radiance through the glass to Simon’s eyes.  As his eyes focused, the heat of the car rose through the air and in a mirror Simon could see the sun's effects on his forehead.  The windowpanes had left a mark across his brow to his hairline. A light pink wash across his forehead was all that remained from his rest.  In the crevice of the windowpanes blocking me the light was left an intersection of two lines, both crossing just above his brow.
As Simon’s eyes focused the boxcar's features from repeated patterns in the dark red carpets to the lackluster shine of the tarnished silver coating on all the door handles and armrests. There were a few unknown faces in the boxcar. Although warm Simon didn't recognize the faces he noticed a man towards the other end of the car.  His eyes simmered with tears and his shoulders lay slouched, pushed down by some unseen burden. The clothing, by which he wore, simplistic in nature, hung loosely on his arms and swayed on his bones and swayed on his thin legs with the rocking of the train.  His skin seemed darker than any other of the men in this car.  He gazed out the window and sighed.  Simon could not find a visible source for this mans sorrow. The terrain on which this train traveled seemed rough, dry, parched earth. From what Simon guessed he was either in the southwest United States or Kansas.
The fumes traveled swiftly over the ground when this train would rumble by.  The air was thick with the stench of the train’s impure exhaust.  You can hear the rails moaning underneath the energy of this steel beast.  This train has no course, it has no destination, and it has but one objective to complete, to run its passengers forever to an unassigned finale, never stopping always running with the sun.
Simon has forgotten exactly when he joined this assembly of unholy steel and metal.  Although he does remember yesterday but not the day before, he was signing the papers of his father’s estate.  Signing away his entire father’s estate to his siblings, who stood by the coffin waiting for Simon, and each planning their own financial futures?  Simon signed his legal name and soon his accounts would fill and so would his mourning brothers and sisters.  For his fathers many children Simon were not always his fathers’ favorite, thrown out to the street by his father for irreconcilable differences.  Simon would not allow his preferences to bent for his father or that red green mixture that soon would be gracing Simons bank notices.  Simon was banned from his fathers’ sight.  Time passed and so did his father prejudices.  Simon was now welcome in his father’s home and to his father’s side Simon found his plate full of his habit father’s wealth and numbed himself to feelings of icy truth love. After Simons father’s health began to fade, Simon grew strong with is fathers wealth and blood.

His father made his fortune in silver.  
Simon’s father owned numerous mints and mines.  Simon’s share of his fathers’ blood amounted to fifteen silver mines in Mexico, ten mines of the same nature embedded in the Swiss Alps, and another five lost in the peaks and valleys of various Asian countries.  A total of thirty mines, thirty pieces of what his father was Simon signed away what remained of his soul and humanity.
Now Simon finds himself on this train.  He can't remember purchasing a ticket and cannot find a conductor to question this train’s destination.
The other various passengers all see this boxcar with different eyes and with all their separate points of view. The colors seem lighter to Henry.  Henry has not a recollection of any other memory but this car.  He remembers when the dark man in the corner, arrived.  Henry was watching the repeating Henry when he looked up and saw the dark man staring blankly out the window by which he always sat. The face looked the same then as it does now, blank expression, a regretful stare from those simmering eyes.  Henry couldn't find the strength to stand and deliver a greeting to any of the other men who have entered the car since then.  There have been four that have gained access to the car since the dark man. John, Thomas, Clarence, and Simon being the last to join this silent oil panting of a train ride.

Thomas hasn't woken since he arrived; most of the men who have arrived or appeared awaken within a few moments of time. Thomas has been snoring for as much time as John has been awake in the car.  John has been awake the entire time since he has arrived, with a wild, strange stare of remorse and disbelief. John has not spoken a word; just his stare has been his proclamation to the other passengers.  John’s silence has sounded its voice four times since he has been present.  Clarence has been engulfed in a book that he found in his seat, " The Adventures of Tom Sawyer." He has not looked upon since the first page turned and strangely enough remained frozen on that very page since.
Simon heard a harsh grinding noise.  John grunted with discomfort, Thomas rolled to his window and drooled, Clarence turned one page in his book.  A single tear slid down the dark mans cheek and was soaked up by his collar. Only Simon questioned the stability of the train on which they were traveling.
From a soft moan to a gentle scratching, to an abrasive percussion that surrounded the five souls on board this specter of a train. The men soon broke from each their own trances, save one, Thomas remained sound asleep.  The men soon found themselves engulfed by the constant grinding of which they all suffered. The grinding became so loud that Simon covered his ears the sound was horrible.
Others only felt the increased rocking of the train-Side to side the train shook from a gentle rhythmic rocking to a violent abusive tremor that sent Simon headlong into the side of the car with his feet in the air. The dark man gripped his seat and tears flowed freely now. Clarence turned pages one after another hardly even reading the words embedded there in.  Blood flowed from john's bit lip and stained his china bright crimson.  He had a crazy insane strange look in his eye as the train car lifted from it's path and tumbled end over end and shattered it's windows and ripped it's seats from the bolts and tossed these poor souls from it's ragged embrace to all their own silent crashes.
In this wreckage under twisted steel and the remains of these gentlemen’s transparent selves there lay Thomas unharmed and dark man breathing lightly.  His eyes fluttered briefly and opened to see the dark man reading a book, sitting quietly while John dripped tears down his cheeks, not many just one or two. Clarence gripped the arms of his seat and stared frightfully out his only window. Simon rolled to one side and sleep despite the slight vibrations that Thomas felt.
Suddenly Thomas flashed to his childhood a plane ride he and his mother were taking to see his grand mothers funeral. Thomas sat quietly while the other children played with their toys and fought over who got to play the good guy.  Thomas imagined his supposed father a pilot in the war, who supposedly was shot down or lost in battle, either way Thomas had a picture for his father and it was turning yellow with age.  Also he had only one memory of him, one which his mother constantly reminding him of, it was of the last time he even saw his father, late at night and in the doorway of his small nursery.  He wasn't wearing any uniform and no it wasn't a hero’s goodbye.  Crying, and a lot of yelling was the atmosphere of Thomas’s father's exit, not a pleasant I’ll be back phrase.  No I love you dear.  No kiss on the cheek for his mother, rather a slap or a punch on the jaw.  Thomas’s mother constantly swore that he went off to war and that he loved his family.  She said that he forgot to take his lucky charm. It was Thomas’s first tooth.  Mother carried in a small handkerchief in her wallet all the time.
Thomas vomited on the dark red carpet staining it to the usual shade of orange and yellow.  The others gave no reaction to Thomas’s actions. Only the rhythmic rocking of the car com­forted Thomas in almost a strange way. Scratching, a faint very faint scrape.  Thomas’s eyes widened and his eyes scanned the car, was alone there was no other man in the car.  Just four poor souls with their own focuses.  All with different fears.  All with different stories to tell-All with different pasts to hide.
Soon a slight scrape begat the unfamiliar grinding and Thomas’s eyes have grown wide with the strange feeling that seems so familiar to him, but alien at the very same time.  The rocking begins and Simon rolls across his seat to hit his head on the windowsill and rocks with the movement of the train.  John’s tears have turned his eyes red and his muscles flex to contain his fears.  The dark man has stopped reading.  Simon is stirring in his sleep and the train tips without the virtue of sound to the ear.
Launching each man his own way and cradling Clarence in a bed of twisted steel and remnants of the train.  He stirs, the poor boy stirs, and in his eyes he beholds the boxcar.
