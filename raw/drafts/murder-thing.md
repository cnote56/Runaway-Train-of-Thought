Murder-Thing.                                 Approx. 2,200 words
Cole Dowden

5922 Crown St.

Capitol Heights, MD 20743

cole.dowden@gmail.com


I have, just now, beat the brains out of my hopes and dreams for the third time this morning. I am not embellishing. Out of the closet in my office a ragged and limping hulk of everything I ever hoped and dreamt stumbles and crashes out, as if on cue by some existential playwright.

No doubt my brain had stirred this thing before me. I had just read a story about a man that found fame and fortune from doing what was most difficult for so many. He made something out of nothing and sold it to all that would purchase. He was the focus of the article that praised his genius, command, and now great fortune of what he djinned from nothingness. This Thing would be my modest equivalent.

The author of the article described the minimalism of the man’s office with beautiful details of the clean lines and sparse embellishments. I looked around at my desk and saw a crowded workspace filled with stress balls, a desk fan, a little statue of three Buddhas in seeing no evil, hear no evil, and speak no evil poses. I counted three cups and containers filled with pens, markers, and highlighters. I type everything I work on, why do I need so many damned writing instruments?  Two letter openers. I rarely receive any letters. Everything is in an email these days. Why do I need two goddamned letter openers?!

The Thing grunts at me; impatient bastard cannot wait for my attention.

I turn away from my morning work: job applications, email, and computer with my focus-music droning in the background to face this mis-mashed hulk.

Interstellar Overdrive from  Pink Floyd’s Piper at the Gates of Dawn is playing on my music stream. The Internet is the holder, conveyor, and betrayer of all things these days so it might as well give me some cacophony of late-60’s psychedelic rock to urge this violence along.

It shudders when I whip my head around as it grunts at me from across the room. It has one good eye; the other socket holds a telescoping lens with a red laser range-finder hanging out of it. No doubt a holdover from some rabid vision of a great science-fiction space opera I wanted to nurture and whore for a fortune and my little article about my genius. Never wrote that story down. Although it lives somewhere in my brain it must have found someplace to grow on this...thing.

Its hair is similar to mine; brown, boring, combed over the reversing hairline that matches my father’s scalp. Under that scalp is a skull that likely resembles a shape similar to mine. Of all the times I have butchered this thing, I never bother to complete the autopsy and study this mongrel. Today I will take the time to peel back its skin and see if it has the little ridge of skull bone along the left side - as I have. We have so much in common and uncommon between us; I feel it is just prudent to learn all I can about this… thing.

Its ears look like mine. Even down to the one mole I noticed on my right earlobe when I was 13 and trying to justify growing my hair longer than my mother would allow, you know to prevent skin cancer - I didn’t believe it either back then. I also wanted a mohawk the next season and a leather jacket. Never got either of those wishes to come true so they must have found root on this...thing.

Its shoulders hunch and roll forward, the left shoulder bulges more over the trapezius muscle, and the deltoids are more defined. No doubt this was a relic of all my heroic play-acting when I was a 10-year-old boy playing by himself on a 64-acre farm that did not grow anything other than organic hay and trees his step-grandfather refused to cut unless they fell or died of “natural causes.” The 10-year-old version of myself liked to fashion nunchucks out of wire and bamboo and “katana swords” from split tree branches and old broomsticks. The motions and exercise must have found some germination on the bones of this...thing.

Its forearms long and sinewed covered in dark, wiry arm - undoubtedly contoured form hours of flexing and wrenching on its genitals for years on end. I blame this malformation directly on God. He gave this thing thumbs, elbows, and the natural tendency of fear. Everything a growing boy needs to find a natural reaction revolting and the embarrassment to do everything he could to hide, shush, and wrestle it away from sight.  The custom must have been pleasing to the sense of this...thing.

It limps and grunts toward me as I whip my chair around to face it. The bent knees and dropped foot no doubt come from those glory days spent under the stadium lights. I stand with a letter opener in my hand from my desk chair and feel the pop in my right knee - it shudders and with a gnarled paw of a human hand rubs at its right knee in response. The step I take toward it causes a twinge of pain at the connection between my right hip and spinal column - in reality, a mess of scar tissue, bone spurs, and inflamed muscles. This monster growls and steels itself for the coming battle - despite the pain, it feels at its core.

My pain; it’s pain. 
My hate; it's hate. 
My hope; it's hope.
My loss; it’s loss.

My battle… you get the picture no need to drone on. I have to beat the brains out of its skull now. Please hold and enjoy the “music.”

###

I never wipe the blood off my face when I am done. It finds a way to disappear. I never bother to remove the body of the Thing. I step over the corpse until it either rots and fades to dust. Sometimes it takes minutes. Other times it takes months, even a year once. That was a terrible time to spend in this office. The smell made me gag, but none of my house visitors ever commented or responded. I guess their noses never registered the response to its post-mortem stimuli.

My wife does not seem to notice it either. But she rarely comes into the office at the house. She has a desk alongside mine, but she does not use it much. It holds a pile of papers and old mail for now. She works at an office and has a desk there; I don’t blame her for not wanting to come home and sit at a desk where she sleeps. Me? I am an unemployed writer and formerly trained lawyer and journalist. I am always working because once you become either a lawyer or writer, you are not either of those things.

But back to the corpse - that’s more interesting, right? 
I’ve propped it up in the desk chair at my wife’s desk. I did not want to bother stepping over it when I refilled my coffee after the battle. Its eyes are closed. I closed them with a bit of tape from my desk. I hate to leave them open and staring into the air above my head. In its demise, it soiled itself. Every time like clockwork and it stinks. I do not know what it could eat, but every time I beat its brains from its skull it shits and pisses itself like it is the day after Thanksgiving.

I see a bit of ashiness beginning to form on its fingers. I think it will disappear soon, so I have to act quickly.

My pen knife - although dull - will do for this work. Mental note, purchase a scalpel for the next fight. I have to peel back the scalp to study the skull - or what left of it. A rough saw of the paling and now grey skin will have to be quick as the knuckles are beginning to disappear. Its shoes are starting to dissolve into nothing.

Damn you, slow down! You pig fucker!

I just now got the scalp pulled back and the hair I gripped fluttered from existence, and the corpse slumped to the floor. Why?! Why do you have to go so quickly this time?! Why, did I not do this for that year so long ago?!

Its legs disappeared along with the supporting mass holding it in the chair. Of all the times for you go, now you have to beat the hasty retreat?

It's beginning to crumble now; like stale cake on a kitchen counter. I try to grip its shoulder, and it is like grabbing sand; hold it tighter and the more it slips away. Well, damn you then! Go back into the closet!

I kicked at the disappearing mass. My foot flying through the empty air the force of my kick taking my balance off and a twinge of pain shot through my right hip, down my sciatic nerve. The Thing is gone now. No trace. No body. No blood. No corpse. Nothing to prove or disprove my madness. I grunt my dissatisfaction and fold the penknife back closed. With a toss, it clatters on top my keyboard and bounces into the clutter of my desk.

I suck a breath through my teeth. There is no smell. No scent. Just my office, my desk, my books, and me.

The music has stopped streaming from my speakers.

The coffee in my cup is cold. I drink it down in one gulp. The cold bitterness washes across my tongue back teeth and down my throat.

I’ve still got a few job applications to send this morning before I can justify my unemployment check this week. Thank god for that at least. A few pennies of what I used to be worth; a safety-net from our blue-dog democratic state unemployment system. Thank God for it.

I sit back at my desk. No music this time. No distractions. I type and click and search and send out through the wires my digital hopes and pleas for steady work and a paycheck to keep the lights on.

I push my chair back from my desk and stand up. The pains and twinges of old injuries and former versions of myself echo in my joints. I take my now empty coffee cup in hand. And walk toward the door of the room. Just to the right of the door is the closet; that’s where it comes from.

I stand in front of it. My coffee cup gripped in the palm of my left hand. I reach for the doorknob with my right hand and yank the door open. Darkness.

I tug the pull cord for the closet light and find a row of suit jackets and clutter along the floor. It is not there. I pull the string for the light to help me find if it's hiding in the darkness. Nothing, just suits and old computer parts. I guess you’re not ready for the next round.

I leave the closet door open and exit my office. I’m done trying to find work. I’m done sending my resume to email addresses and filling out job applications online. For a moment I think I should write all this down and try to sell it, but then I feel more empty inside than I’ve felt in long time. I’ve never had much confidence in my writing. I guess that’s why I got a degree in journalism. Then law school. Two stops on a long road that finds me here - standing in my empty house.

The events of this morning happen more often now than they used to. I used to face off with it in my office at the law firm. I used to see it creeping when I worked at the newspaper. Maybe I’m writing this down now just to get it off my chest. I hope they never read it. They’ll think I’ve lost my mind.  Good luck trying to find work from the madhouse, bub.

I retreat to my guitar, in the corner of my bedroom; maybe some music will help me reset my energy for the day.

Soon strained melodies with struggling chord changes fill the emptiness of the house. You would think after twenty years of playing this damned instrument, I would be good at it. I’m not, another lost hope and bedraggled dream of a younger version of me.

###

As I cradle the guitar in my hands and on my lap, I see the open closet in my mind’s eye. I remember, I left the closet door open. 
My hands rush and race over the frets and strings of the guitar jabbing at some syncopated and off-kilter version of a 12 bar blues progression. I love playing the blues. I never said I was good at it.

I see in my mind's eye the light bulb’s pull-string swinging in the crowded closet as I play notes and chords on my guitar. I left the light on. I never leave the lights on in a room when I leave.

In my mind's eye, I see a gnarled hand shoot from the row of suit jackets. The light goes out. The closet door slams shut. This Murder-Thing never cared much for my style of blues.
