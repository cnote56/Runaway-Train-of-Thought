  The sources provide several compelling parallels to the account of
  Tablet K.8538, particularly regarding the themes of temporary
  celestial bodies, retrograde or disarranged orbits, and a "sky that

Native American Parallel Accounts

- Two Celestial Objects in the West: A Tewa legend recounts a time when
  villagers saw "two new stars in the west" that began to move east
  across the heavens. This mirrors the appearance of a temporary
  celestial body (the stranger moon) that followed a trajectory
  different from the regular stars.

- The Snaring and Release of Two Lights: In a Zuni myth, Coyote and
  Eagle steal two boxes from the Kachinas containing the Sun and Moon.
  When Coyote opens them, both escape into the sky at once, causing the
  world to shrivel and turn cold. This theme of two major lights being
  present and then "escaping" or changing the environment mirrors the
  dual-lunar presence described on K.8538.

- Disintegration and Rain of Fire: The theme of a celestial body
  breaking apart and burning the world is widespread.

  - The Southern Ute tell of a hero who struck the sun, breaking off a
    piece that touched the ground and "set fire to the world".

  - The Maya (Popol Vuh) and tribes in Mexico record a time when the sky
    rained a "sticky substance" or bitumen and the face of the earth
    grew dark with a "great din of fire".

  - The Pawnee believe that in the past, the "South Star" left its place
    to look at the North Star, and that future destruction will be
    signaled by the moon turning red and stars falling to the earth.

- The Solid/Lowered Sky: Many tribes, including the Snohomish and Kaska,
  describe a time when the "sky was very low" and required a collective
  effort (shouting "Yahu!") to push it up. This coincides with
  interpretations that cosmic dust or gases from a passing body (like
  the stranger moon or a comet) once obscured the heavens.

Indian (Hindu/Vedic) and Eastern Parallel Accounts

- The Maruts (The Terrible Ones): The Hindu Vedas describe the Maruts as
  a host of "terrible ones" who shine like snakes and fires. They are
  seen "hurling the stone in the flight" and creating darkness even by
  day, mirroring the meteor showers and "burning stones" described in
  the Sumerian aftermath.

- The "Seven Suns" and Second Suns: Buddhist sacred texts
  (Visuddhi-Magga) mention that after a great deluge, a "second sun
  appeared". This body caused incessant heat and the eventual burning of
  the world, mirroring the brightness and destructive finale of the
  Sumerian stranger moon.

- Two Shepherds and Retrograde Motion: Outside of Sumerian texts, an
  Akkadian clay prism from Kish dated to 2093 BCE (the same year as the
  tablet's conclusion) describes "two shepherds" in the sky, with the
  younger one "walking backwards". This is a direct parallel to the
  retrograde orbit recorded on K.8538.

Subjects for Further Research

  Based on the sources, these specific areas warrant deeper
  investigation to bridge the K.8538 account with global myths:

- Hoerbiger’s Theory of Satellites: This theory, mentioned by von
  Däniken, suggests a captured satellite slowed Earth's rotation before
  disintegrating and being replaced by the current moon. An ancient idol
  discovered in 1932 reportedly records this satellite making 425
  revolutions in a year of 288 days.

- The "Face of Hammered Copper" Motif: K.8538 describes the moon as
  having a copper-like face. Researching other cultures that describe a
  "copper moon" or "metallic sun" during periods of darkness (such as
  the red moon in Pawnee legends) may provide more technical links.

- The Era of Nabonassar and Calendrical Shifts: Multiple sources
  indicate ancient years were 360 days long before shifting to 365.25
  days. Researching the exact timing of these shifts in the Mayan
  Tzolkin versus Babylonian astronomical diaries may reveal if they
  coincide with the stranger moon's 11-month orbital disruption.

- The "Sky that Wept Fire" (2093 BCE): Geological core samples from
  Mesopotamia show a distinct impact layer from this exact year,
  containing microscopic spherules. Cross-referencing this with the
  "Fire Sun" age of the Mayas or the "Fire-Rain" age of the Toltecs
  (Quiauh-tonatiuh) could establish a global dating for the event.
