In quiet possibly the most hypocritical moment, the NFL brass has seen fit to passive aggressively shut down player featured fantasy football events held at casinos around the country. While player conduct is an area of concern it is hypocrisy to shut these players out of a marketing and branding event. Why did the NFL do it? Dallas Cowboy Quarterback and full-time Texas Swoon Machine Tony Romo said it best via Rant Sports.

“It’s like when you’re in high school, and you don’t get invited to the party, it makes you feel bad,” Romo said in an interview. “If they really wanted to just be a part of it, all they had to do was call and ask. If would have been a lot easier than going about the process the way they did.”

Yeah, that's pretty much par for course, with the NFL. Unless they get a cut then nobody gets a cut. Gee, that sounds an awful lot like the way a small group of Italian-American guys in the burrows of New York, New Jersey, and Boston once held out as a business plan. To speak more, plainly, I am talking about the Mob. The Mafia. Omerta. FIFA. Wait scratch that last one. But not really.

And this is perfectly fine. You see, it's market and brand protection. And like any business or corporation, the NFL has the power to walk into a place and shut it down if it's brand hasn't been licensed or paid for or any number of reasons. And there is little to be done about it.

To illustrate, let's say, your dear old Grandpappy is in the hospital. He's been a Jets fan all his life. Aside from you, his life has thereby been filled with a lot of disappointment; because he's a Jets fan.

Let's say to cheer him up the Nurses draw a Jets logo on the whiteboard in his hospital room every morning. He wakes up sees today's date, the name of his nurse on duty, and his beloved Jets logo. In fact, since he's been seeing this logo every morning his health condition has improved and the Doctor's think he'll make a full recovery.

But back to the logo, it is expertly drawn and meticulous with detail. A logo drawn so good in fact, that NFL Executive Godger Roddell notices it from across the hallway. He is there visiting a registered organ donor that he plans on harvesting as soon as the flatline hits. Mr. Roddell is shocked to see the expertly crafted Jets logo that he tweets his legal army into action.

Suddenly, a Corporate Attorney fast ropes through your Grandpappy's hospital room window; glass shattering all around. Pulls from his briefcase a bundle of official looking papers and nails the papers to the whiteboard. And with the click of another billable hour, the Corporate Attorney is out of the room and down the hall with a job well done.

The papers were a cease and desist. And Grandpappy isn't looking good after all the commotion.

So, in short, the Non-Profit Football League can swoop in and tell its players to avoid certain events. It's all within their domain, within the guise of market and brand protection. I don't blame them. They have a multi-billion cash cow pumping out cash 24/7. If I were charged with keeping the cash cow alive over Grandpappy; I'm sorry but Grandpappy still refers to Joe Namath as 'Broadway Joe."

But getting all this back to the players and the NFL being heavy handed in quasi-corporate-parentage. Them's the breaks, Romo. And Romo knows breaks. This theoretically counts against fantasy football. At least if an NFL player is in the room. The NFL couched its disapproval with the fact that the event was to be held at a casino, but that is gossamer dress in a rain storm. Like Romo said, they didn't get invited to the party so they shut it down.

I'm glad the Non-Profit Football League is taking care of its image and shutting these rouge football players out of marketing and developing their own band based on their athletic prowess. I'm really happy that we can sleep better at night knowing the conduct off the field isn't intruding on the sanctity on the field, like for instance during an AFC Championship game earlier this year.

Till next time, I like my sugar with coffee and cream.
