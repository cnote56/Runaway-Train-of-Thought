The Havana Electric Railway, also known as the Hershey Railway, is a
standard-gauge electric interurban railway that runs from Casablanca in
Havana to the city of Matanzas, approximately 92 kilometers (57 miles)
to the east. Here are some key details about the railway:

- Construction and Ownership: The railway was built by The Hershey
  Company to transport sugar to the port of Havana. It is currently
  owned and operated by Ferrocarriles de Cuba.

- Route: The railway runs from Casablanca to Matanzas, with several
  intermediate stops, including the town of Camilo Cienfuegos, formerly
  known as Hershey.

- Electrification: The railway is the only surviving electric line in
  Cuba, operating at 600 V DC.

- Historical Significance: The original electric interurban cars were
  bought from the JG Brill Company, but these were replaced by
  60-year-old cars from the Ferrocarrils de la Generalitat de Catalunya
  in the 1990s.

- Frequency: There are usually three trains per day, each way between
  Havana Casablanca and Matanzas, meeting at Hershey Station, the
  midpoint.

- Tourism: The railway is a tourist attraction, offering a unique
  glimpse into Cuba’s sugar industry history and rural life.
