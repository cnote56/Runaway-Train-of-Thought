# Chapter Four: The Lazarus Port

The world split in two, and neither half was sane.

**[Physical: Havana Hangar]**
My chest heaves, a jagged, wet rattle. My eyes are open in the dark tunnel, but I see nothing but the gray concrete and the encroaching boots of the BIC cleanup team. My hand is a dead weight, but I can feel the trigger of a dropped facility-turret pressed against my palm. I pull it. *Thwump-thump-thump.* The recoil jars my shoulder, a flare of agony so bright it turns the dark tunnel into a kaleidoscope of red stars. I’m not aiming; I’m just cutting the room in half with high-velocity lead.

**[Digital: The Root]**
I am striding across a bridge of liquid obsidian. The BIC Watchers are shrieking—not audio, but pure, high-frequency data-pain that shreds my avatar’s skin. I reach out, and my fingers are long, ancient, calloused things. I know the feeling of a heavy fountain pen. I know the smell of a dry vault in 1973. I am Dooling, and I am the man who built the walls.

**[Physical: Havana Hangar]**
The cleanup team is pinned. I’m firing the turret, but I’m not the one doing it. My brain is a static-filled TV. I hear Coach Henderson screaming about the snap, and I hear my grandfather whispering about *Financial Osmosis*. I am on the floor, drooling, shivering, my body twitching in time with the turret’s vibration. A bullet tears through the wall inches from my ear. I don't flinch. I’m too busy calculating the dividend yield of the BIC’s entire infrastructure.

**[Digital: The Root]**
I reach the jagged tower. The Watchers dive, their geometric bodies unfolding like serrated knives. I don't run. I *reconcile*. I reach into their code and find the "debt" in their programming. I delete their reason for existing. They don't die; they simply... balance out to zero. They vanish into the amber light. I am not Dooling or the Grandfather. I am the *Audit*. I am the inevitable conclusion of the ledger.

**[Synthesis]**
The pain in my shoulder is the same as the pain of a corrupted data-packet. The smell of the hangar’s ozone is the same as the smell of the Obsidian Towers. 

*I am both.*

When I need to punch through a wall, I use the Grandfather’s cold, calculated architecture. When I need to feel the fear of the cleanup team, I use Dooling’s raw, panicked survival instinct. 

I look at my hands. In the hangar, they are bloodied and gray. In the Root, they are gold and shimmering with static. 

"They don't know who they're fighting," I say, my voice sounding like a symphony of iron and static.

The cleanup team enters the tunnel. They see me—Dooling, the twitching, bloody mess on the floor. The leader raises his rifle, his face a mask of professional, cold intent.

He doesn't know that three hundred feet above him, in the Root, I am currently reaching into his bank account and vaporizing his existence.

"Check your vitals," I whisper, a wide, delirious grin splitting my face.

The guard stops. His eyes go wide as his suit’s tactical HUD begins to scream with system failures. He isn't being hacked; he’s being *erased*.
