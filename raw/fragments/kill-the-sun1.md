Kill The Sun

How to Ruin Everything and still get paid.

A Scien-Fiction Theorem on the Fallacy of Man.

By Cole Dooling


## The Bitter End


The driving wild and bitter cold pressed in through the cracked rubber seals between the metal hull plates and internal electrical ducts and conduits. The wild and cold were unrelenting these cycles.

And there was no avoiding it anymore.

Fifty years of driving cold blizzard-like conditions coupled with brief spring-like thaws and then incredibly robust and violent summer months with shifts in the weather so slight it makes 30-degree shifts in a single day seem mundane. All of these forces took a toll on everything subjected to it.

Inside from the snow and cold, Brady sits in his office chair wrapped in a thick blanket around his coated shoulders with his fingerless gloves and fleshy tips poking out of mounds of blankets and fabric.

VideoScreen:

“Scientists from Harvard and Yale hypothesize a solution to global warming may be as simple as turning down the heat. A highly respected group of meteorologists and planetary scientists have all agreed to explore the concept of controlling the nuclear fission of the sun…”

The room was cast in a blue-green glow from the monitor behind the brightness on Brady’s face.

Janet watched Brady click through the archives. He was swimming in the early 2010s or somewhere before the heat came.

“Why are you watching those history files?” Janet, a geologist by study and wife to Brady by choice, entered the room with a tray of ice samples. Her thick coat zipped halfway up.

“I don’t know,” Brady sighed and swiped the video file away from his screen. “I guess I keep thinking if we just go back as far as we can, we might find something in their failures, before they were successful, on how we might be able to reverse what they did.”

“Not a bad theory, but old news reports don’t get you anywhere worth going. Back then, they barely read a word's first three letters before deciding to finish reading the whole word. Elections were lost because people did not bother to read the whole name. Do you think their news reports were any more reliable?”

Brady grumbled and fished through a basket of data plugs.

“MAY I BE OF SERVICE?”

The Voice startled both of them. The ice samples clattered on the tray as Janet slapped them to the counter.

It was EDGAR.

Rather he preferred, E-D-G-A-R.

The acronym EDGAR highlights artificial intelligence as an evolving and general decision-making technology powering innovations in robotics and beyond. The acronym encapsulates the broad scope and transformative potential of AI.

###

In Unison, “SHUT-UP, EDGAR!”

It was not a happy throple.

EDGAR was an Atficial Inteligence protocol that lived on the geothermal powered networks that’s spotted the surface and subsurfaces of the Earth.

He was a painfully intrusive component of this modern world between Janet and Brady.

“You’ve already got the personal notes of half the original team. Nothing in there, right?” Jan turned away and rubbed her hands together, trying to warm them as he prepared her ice samples for cataloging.

The power failed; everything went dark.

“Well, there goes the generators,” Brady said and fluffed his coat, trying to keep his warmth from dissipating.

“Yeah, so this is how it all will end up?” Jan asked.

The two of them had already consolidated their folds of coats and cloaks into sandwich layers of sorts, one blanket wrapped into another wrapped over into another like the folds of a rose.

“Yeah, with a whimper I guess.” Brady did the mental math as he spoke. “We’ve got roughly 24 hours worth of stasis in the habitat. No doors fly open or major breaches to the outter skin and we’ll be just slowly getting colder over th enext day or so.”

“And then,” Jan asked.

“And then we get to take the last of this and settle in for a long winters nap.” Brady produced a sealed bottle of whiskey from under his folds of blankets and coats.

“You’re solution is to drink our selves to death?” Jan gafawed.

“Well, yeah.” Brady said plainly, “Ive done the numbers, Jan. There is not much else to do. We wont be able to head anythign that we have left that could be eaten. THe generators are out. Kaput. Finito. The temperature out there is too low for the damn combustion cores even to ignite. So, we might as well have a drink and enjoy the most boring show on earth.”

And it would be the most boring show on earth. This was the last gasp of the human race. Sipping whiskey and waiting for the cold to overtake them.

## LONG AGO, before we fucked everything up.


It's pretty unlikely there were any human inhabitants in southern Oregon to witness Mount Mazama's cataclysmic eruption 7,700 years ago.

Archaeological evidence indicates the first people migrated to the Oregon region around 13,000-15,000 years ago after the eruption.

In the days and weeks before the eruption, the ground shook violently more and more as magma moved underground. Steamy vents and geysers may have emerged around the mountain.

Water vapor and abundant steam from heated groundwater and magma. Not toxic, but tell that to scores of wildlife suddenly engulfed in the hot breath of mother earth as the animal is flash boiled alive and then burned as it dips into the maw of the earth below.

Carbon dioxide. Invisible death. The sleeping odorless gas collected and flowed like liquid down the mountain into any crevasses or caves sheltering anything alive. No more. Nothing but death reaps fruit here.

Sulfur dioxide - Pungent, irritating gas that can cause breathing problems at high concentrations. Also reacts with water to produce acid rain.

- Hydrogen sulfide - Extremely hazardous gas with a rotten egg smell. Highly corrosive and toxic even at low levels. Can cause eye and respiratory irritation, nausea, headaches, and even death with exposure.

- Hydrochloric acid - Corrosive, acidic gas that can cause skin, eye, and lung irritation and damage. Formed when hydrothermal waters interact with magmatic gases.

- Hydrogen fluoride - Highly corrosive and toxic gas that can damage lungs and bones. Causes skin and eye burns on contact.

- Carbon monoxide - Odorless, toxic gas that prevents oxygen absorption in the body. Can be fatal at high concentrations.

- Sulfuric acid aerosols - Droplets of corrosive sulfuric acid that can damage eyes, skin, and lungs. Formed when sulfur dioxide interacts with atmospheric moisture.

The concentrations and mixes of these gases around volcanic fissures and vents depend on the volcano's activity and can fluctuate rapidly. Generally staying away from visible fume releases is wise to avoid health hazards. Monitoring volcanic gases provides crucial insights into a volcano's subsurface processes and advance warning of eruptions.

- As the volcano rumbled to life, huge columns of ash and gas would havestarted billowing tens of thousands of feet into the sky over the course of hours or days. The sky would have darkened and the sun blocked out.

- Pyroclastic flows - fast-moving currents of hot gas, ash and rock - would have barreled down the mountain at over 100 mph, incinerating anything in their path for tens of miles. Deadly ash would have rained down everywhere.

- When the volcano fully exploded, the eruption column could have reached as high as 50 miles into the atmosphere. Shock waves and ejected volcanic material would have devastated the surrounding landscape for many more miles.

- Ash may have fallen as far as the Midwest and Canada. Any people in the area would have been forced to flee or risk perishing due to falling ash, toxic gases or lava flows. It would have seemed like the end of the world.

- In the aftermath, the area was transformed with over 30 cubic miles of ejected material and a massive crater 6 miles wide and half a mile deep - today's breathtaking Crater Lake.
