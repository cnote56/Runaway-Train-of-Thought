Jack the Reaper

What if the greatest serial killer of all time was every serial killer?


# Synopsis:


Duke Albert is a historian of serial killers. Rich from writing royalties and the fascinating analysis behind the facts and data of the history of murder and the thinking behind the eyes of the killer. He also lives in the 22nd century in a luxurious ring above the super-city of London.

He hides a terrible truth.

He is not only an expert on serial killers - he is the greatest serial killer of all time.

He is every serial killer in time.

A man who satisfies his bloodlust through time travels to th worst cities across time to indulge his passion for murder.

Duke Albert has a time machine and hides his crimes in history.

He is openly known to Londoners as Jack the Ripper, The Spring Healed-Jack, and H.H. Holmes to Americans.

But to all, he is Jack the Reaper, the Killer Across Time.

In this future, Duke Albert chose to live above London, the city, he was born one-hundred and seventy-seven years ago.

Duke Albert’s exoskeleton whirled his legs into position and moved them along with their support to his upper body “walked”  - more floated since the gears and gyroscopic balancers in his exoskeleton were of th finest design and craftsmanship. ## 1888.


This is the story of two monsters. One in one timeline that we all know, and in its second mutation, the hero we fear.

The perfect murder didn’t occur until well into the 21st century. A full 100 years after time travel was proven to exist and then monetized by the protagonist of this strange tale.

In a mansion in East London, sometime near 1866, a monster was born, From birth, the strain of his entrance into this world killed his mother. A young bride to an old man gifted by the whims of a king with a disgusting amount of unearned wealth and power. This old man, now father, Duke of someplace long gone in your timeline, was staring dumbfounded at the reality before him this October 31st, 1866. The lighting outside flashed over the candlelight in the bedroom like photographing a crime scene. A pale young bride - long bled out in the marital bed.

The doctor gasped as bloodied arms, and gore-filled hands struggled to hold a deformed newborn. Its head, shoulders, and arms were large and bulky, but below its hips, the legs twisted and formed a curved like that of a cat’s than a human’s.

A nurse gasped, and a platter of sterilized towels and instruments clattered, knocking a candle stick over, extinguishing half the flame to the room.  As lighting crashed outside, the old man cast his hands to the sky and rushed out of the room. Lighting blinked through the windows casting a stop-motion effect on the room.

The Doctor passed the child to another nurse. Another nurse scrambled to clean the blood cast across the bed and spilled to the floor below the dead mother.

Lighting slowed and blinked the scene to blackness as the doctor wept while wiping gore from his hands.

The nurse hushed the child to her shoulder, exiting the room into a dark corridor.

The mother died just before the child’s first breath.

The old man drank himself dead before the child’s first birthday.
