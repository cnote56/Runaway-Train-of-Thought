The Day The World Changed.
By William C. Dooling III

Light filled the room through the wire mesh inlayed windows. A man sits in
a plastic chair staring out the window at the empty courtyard. His arms are
crossed and his head is half shaved with a long scar running along the side
of his head. Another man with shaggy hair and dingy pajamas waddles closer
to the first. In the approaching man’s hands he clutches a tattered teddy
bear.
      “So…So what’s …your story?” the man holding the teddy bear stutters as
asks the seated man.
      The seated man does not acknowledge the question. Even his eyes do not
blink.  The teddy bear holding man maneuvers into the seated man’s field of
view.
      “I’m Teddy. This is my bear. What’s your name?” he paused and
nervously shifted his weight from foot to foot. “Are you…”
      “Do you remember Poolesville?” the seated man said without looking up
at Teddy.
      Teddy dumbly stood in silence and nervously crushed his teddy bear in
his hands.
      “Sit down,” he said coldly as a fly buzzed around his head.


                                      *
It was 6:30 am
My eyes opened 15 minutes before my alarm was to go off. I laid there in
the comfortable prison of a sleepy bed. My covers half thrown off of my
half naked body. Something was different about the air blowing in from the
open window. The air it tasted like it was laced. Laced with some metallic
additive like impure water from a poisoned well. The morning news rattled
off nothing important, just old news about yesterday. But I knew this
strange taste in the air was news, important news.
My morning shower felt like every morning shower I ever took, but the air
changed everything this morning.

…7:00 am
      Heard on the radio in my car they found a man lying dead on the steps
of the courthouse. They reported nothing too strange about the story just a
man gripping one possession, an open empty thermos. The radio report said
his face was twisted and contorted like he died screaming.
      The drive to my school was a lonely one. It was strange because I
usually had the contentions of traffic to deal with, but instead only a
handful of cars braved these empty streets.
      The metallic taste in the air grew with my advancement towards the
downtown district. I couldn’t see any evidence but I could have sworn there
were bodies-dead bodies lying on the roadsides; lining the streets like
some morbid garnish. I thought they were just the homeless that always
slept on the ground with concrete for their pillows but these didn’t seem
to fit the part.

…7:25 am
      The parking lot of my school was almost empty, unusual because most
of the students arrive long before their eight o’clock classes. The
metallic taste of the air seemed to hang like a bad dream over the center
of town. The buildings seemed to have a strange yellow tint to them and
smelt heavy with the metallic scent.

…7:45 am
      The campus was empty nothing was open and no students were there.
Like a ghost town it was vast and deserted. None of the buildings were
unlocked and even the buildings that did not ever close the doors were
barricaded from the inside.

…8:20 am
      A dog shuffled nervously across the courtyard in front of the
library, its leash dragging behind him. I sat on the steps of the library
my briefcase leaning against my right thigh. My hands clasped as if I were
saying a prayer only my mouth was silent and my mind raced with questions
on why.
      Why was the school locked up tight?
      Why were there no students?
      Why was there such little traffic?
      Why were there so few cars?
      What was this metallic taste in the air?

      The sun rose slowly across the courtyard.

…9:00 am
      Blood dripped from my nose and tapped the side of my briefcase; it
slowly ran from gravity to the stone steps under my feet. Suddenly my nasal
cavities filled, felt like I just had an immense cold awaken in me. Three
drops hit my briefcase before the full rush of blood began to drain. I
cupped my right hand to my face and search my back pocket for my
handkerchief with my left. When I found it I pressed it tightly to my mouth
and nose.
      My nose gushed with blood. I sat motionless with my mind racing while
my hand felt the eerie warmth of my own blood soaking through the
handkerchief.

…10:00 am
      It took and hour for the blood to stop dripping from my nose. I had
the copper taste of it in my mouth and smelled it above the sick metallic
one that hung in the air around me. I decided to walk back to my car and go
to the town newspaper to try and find my friend John. John Guzman was a
reporter for the Main Street Post our only town newspaper. If any one would
know what was happening he would.
      As I walked back across the expanse of the ghost town like campus a
slight breeze picked up into a strong gust and immediately my nose gushed
with blood, again. I cupped my half soiled handkerchief to my face, half
blocking my view of the path that lay ahead of me. My balance suffered from
all the blood loss I was experiencing.
      I tripped over something, half soft-half solid. My hand scraped along
the concrete and my knees struck hardness with my weight tumbling to the
ground. I rolled over clutching the palm of my hand and curling up to
soften the dull pain of my knee. Then I saw what I tumbled over.
      The leash whipped in the gusts of wind and the fur shuffled with a
violent motion. Two pools of blood at opposite ends of the carcass gleamed
in the morning light. One pool from the nose, eyes and mouth of the dogs’
head; the second pool of blood oozed from the anus of the dead animal.
      Shock and sudden fear encased me as I scooted on my backside away
from the horrible sight. I felt a sickness welling up inside me. The warm
sickness shot out of my mouth and nose choking me. It wasn’t all remnants
of food and bile but also a great deal of blood.
      As I choked and spat the remainder of the sickness from my mouth my
eyes scanned, like a frightened animal looking for its predator, this
horrible scene. My briefcase, it was half laying in the southern pool of
the dogs’ blood and half soiled in my own red mess. My mouth gagged as I
gingerly clasped my fingers round the leather handle of it. The blood
shimmered in the early morning sunlight as I lifted the briefcase out of
the pool. A thin stream of the dogs red mess drained of the brown leather
of the briefcase, the wind spraying the last of it in the direction west.

…10:35 am
      The radio in my car crackled static. All of the stations were silent
with only white noise broadcasting. Now I knew where I had to go-the
newspaper office. I had to find John-if John was still there.
      The streets were dotted with abandoned cars. Brake lights glowing and
doors swung open with a mass or two laid out beside it. Felt like I was
driving through a cemetery with the bodies and caskets on top of the earth.

      Up ahead I saw a man stumbling along the sidewalk. His ragged clothes
dark with filth. Before I could get my car up to where he was, he
disappeared into the alleyway between the newspaper office and the
drugstore.
      He lay in a heap atop a pile of garbage bags. His long gray hair
ruffled in the slight breeze. As I crept closer to his body I heard a half
moan half exhale of breath.
      “Sir!” I shouted, “Sir, you okay?”
      My hands shook as I pulled his shoulders up off the garbage pile.
      Blood ran out of his face like a faucet turned on low. His white pale
face frozen and his eyes cold and void of any color but the palest white.
      My hand began to shake as I desperately tried to rub whosever blood
was on my hands off of them. I had to find John.

…11:00 am
      The Newspaper Office.
      Yesterday’s news stared at me, as I wiped a little blood from my
nose, from behind the thin glass of the newspaper machine. What ever
happened today happened early this morning.
      The front window was half smashed through half intact. The front door
was unlocked but a body lay wedged like a doorstop blocking it. Red blood
streaked on the white tile floor as I pushed the door and the body aside
enough for me to squeeze through it.
      A steady breeze pushed through the broken window filling the room
with stale half metallic-half blood stench into the front office as well as
into my nose. The receptionist was at her desk, face down into coffee
stained papers; a red crimson circle stained inside the larger brown one.
Looked like she was aiming at some strange target, which she hit dead
center.
      I stepped slowly through the newsroom. The computer screens were blank
like headstones and each desk looked like a freshly dug grave. Breezes,
through half opened windows, tossed papers into the air; they danced across
the desks like little white devils in hell.
      Johns office door was closed. The iced glass was tinted a dingy yellow
from his cigar smoke. The unlocked door opened easily and the office was
empty. There was no power in the entire town but his desktop printer had a
half page stuck in its jowls. John was not at his desk; a cup of coffee and
a half eaten bagel sat beside his keyboard.
      It was strange reading the last words you best friend ever wrote. He
was covering the latest info about the dead man found on the steps of the
courthouse. Just half a sheet was printed the remainder blank and
unscathed.
      My mouth was dry with thirst. I somehow found the break room after
walking like a specter of death among the bloody scenes of terror like I
was in some haunted house. A water bottle was lying on its side of the
break room floor just inches from the frozen grasp of a dead woman’s hand.
The seal was unbroken but there was a thumbprint of blood on the label-half
obscuring the brand name. I could not stand the burning thirst enough to
forget it so I broke the seal of the bottle and drained the water into me.
      John was in the restroom at the end of the hallway. I found him under
a toilet, his face covered in blood. His body frozen in the contorted pose
of a painful death.
      The mirror startled me. My reflection seemed nothing as I had
remembered it. The whites of my eyes were shrouded with red vessels webbing
them like blood streaked across a white tiled floor. My ashen face
shuttered with every breath that dared move pass my lips. My hands braced
the weight of my body as I leaned closer to my sunken reflection. I watched
as the pupils of my eyes shifted narrow then wide, narrow then wide. My
eyes studied every inch of my face, logging everything from the filth that
somehow trapped itself on my face and streaked across my chin to my cheeks.
A long black mark ran across my forehead from right to left in the
reflection and interceded with the opposing mark that crossed vertically
down my face, both mixing with dried blood that stained out of my nose and
mouth.
      A low static hum came into my ears like there was a tiny radio placed
in the center of my head. My eyes locked on to the reflection of
themselves. My stare froze my self, just like John’s. For a horrific moment
my stare froze itself in the reflection. I do not know how long I stood
there. I know it was a long time but I just can’t be sure.
      The fast thump of a helicopter broke my trance. I heard it pace
overhead and away from me. Leaping through the newsroom I charged out the
throwing it all the way open. A body tripped me as it laid half in the
gutter of the street and half on the sidewalk. The strange thump from the
helicopter was gone and I sat in silence picking small pebbles out of my
forearm. Blood didn’t flow out of the wound in my arm. The wounds weren’t
serious, but I felt the coppery taste of some escaping from my nostrils.
      The helicopter was making fast passes around the outskirts of town; I
could hear the blades make their drumbeat in circles around me like wolves
rounding a campfire at night. I don’t know why but when I finally heard
something other than my own breathing I didn’t want to hear anything but my
own breathing.

…1:15 pm
      My watch ticked away the seconds as I sat motionless waiting to
become like the dead around me, they looked like sprinkles on a cupcake as
they lay in the street.
      The winds pushed air into my face and by default into me. My head
tilted back to see the sun high in the sky like it was hanging from a
celestial noose. Just for a second I wondered if I were still alive. A
muscle in my leg proved to me I was. It twitched like some strange Morse
code telling me to get up and find out what really happened before I die
without a clue.
      City hall was just up the street, east. That’s were I needed to see
where the body was found this morning and what John had spent his last few
minutes writing on.

…1:30 pm
      Police tape fluttered in the wind amidst the lifelessness that lay in
the street, sidewalks, and steps of the courthouse. Everywhere death was
everywhere and I was standing knee deep in it.
      There was no body clutching a thermos on the steps of the city hall
just a chalk outline that seemed queer and out of place with all this
evidence of atrocity around me like the final puzzle piece missing from a
nearly completed puzzle.
      The bodies lay in heaps. People heaped on top off each other like
leaves pilling up in the fall. I felt like a gravedigger just back from
vacation and my work was piled up waiting for me.
      The back half of an ambulance sat wedged out of the front of the
hardware store. The rear doors ajar with medical supplies and a paramedic’s
body spilled out of it. The supplies on the ground pooled out of the open
backside of the van.
      I found him incased in a black body bag still clutching the opened
thermos in his dead hands. His long black hair was streaked gray and white.
His face was painfully contorted. The tendons under his skin were still
stiff with what ever must have caused all this death. He wasn’t homeless;
his teeth and clothes were too clean. His skin was originally olive tan
burnt long ago by the desert heat, but now it was a ghostly white. His
thick lips were stretched thin to the corners of his mouth. The blood
stained lightly on the apexes of his flesh. Half a tongue, split like a
snakes, lie in his cheek; the other half I could only imagine remained held
behind the white teeth of his dead expression.
      The thermos was empty. Whatever was inside had long since evaporated.
Must have been some sort of gas. When he released it, the opening of the
thermos was the last thing he must have seen. He didn’t have any ID on him
no wallet, no papers, all his pockets were empty.

…2:30 pm
      I sat on the curb in front of the hardware store and thought to
myself.
      Why was I alive? If that is the last question I had to answer then I
was not in luck because then I did not have to worry about tomorrow,
because now I didn’t care.

…4:30 pm
      I spent the last two hours driving in my car looking for anybody I
could find. Nothing-nobody-nowhere.
      Nothing moving at city hall.
      Nobody alive at the college.
      Nowhere were there any clues to be found.
…4:40 pm
      In my car the radio had looped continually through the empty airwaves,
the digital display clicking all the way up to 107.9 then beginning again
at 87.7.
      Then the display stopped on 99.9, the static sputtered out of the
speakers for brief moment. I stopped the car and stared in awe at the
radio. A voice came over the airwaves.
      “Attention!” the voice sounded through the emptiness of my car.
      “Attention! This is the Federal Emergency Management Agency.
Transmitting on frequency 99.9. If you are receiving this broadcast please
do not be alarmed. The greater Poolesville area has been attacked with a
chemical or biological weapon. Please make your way to the high school
football stadium on the west side of town. The attacks chemicals have
dispersed enough to allow for the survivors to travel safely outside.
Please make your way to the High School football stadium on the west side
of town. This message will repeat.”
      Blood dripped from my nose as the message looped over and over itself
echoing into my skull. At first a slow drip then a trickle all leading to a
stream of blood coming out of my head. I didn’t care. I wasn’t going to the
football stadium. If it was so safe here in the town to travel then why
wasn’t the National Guard rolling through the street like I was. Why hadn’t
the helicopter landed in front of the city hall? Where was the Red Cross?
Why would they want to corral the survivors up in a stadium? Perhaps the
blood loss had made me delusional maybe a little paranoid, but I wasn’t
going to the stadium. I wasn’t going to be put behind a chain link fence-
sanitized, questioned, studied, poked and prodded. I wasn’t going to do
that to myself. I wasn’t going to let them do that to me. If I was to die,
then I was going to die on my terms, in my house, in my bed not on a
football field.
      One man whom I didn’t know already tired to kill me today. I wasn’t
going to let a group of unknowns finish his job. If they had a vaccine or
treatment they would have said something in the broadcast.
      The rapid thump of the helicopter pounded its way across the sky. For
a brief moment I thought they might be following me. Then I realized I
didn’t care. I was going home to die.

…5:15 pm
      The steering wheel curved under the palms of my hands. The bumps of
the grip seemed to tickle them as I turned the wheel following the path
home for the last time.
      The blood was soaking through my shirt. I could feel the warm
moisture slowly chill on my chest and I didn’t bother looking down to see
the evidence. My driveway was just ahead on the left. I parked my car half
on the street half in the yard. I walked slowly with the sound of the
helicopter pacing my steps to the front door.

…6:55 pm
      The sun’s last rays of this terrible day glowed outside my front
window. I sunk down slowly into the black leather of my easy chair and
freed my feet form the confines of my shoes. For a moment I sat bathed in
the sweet warmth of the sunlight pouring into my front living room.
      My vision blurred slightly must have been from the blood loss. Then
my front door creaked open. A figure dressed in a camouflaged chemical suit
and a black gas mask stepped through. Before I lost my vision to the
blackness I heard the words.
      “We found him.”

…Sometime later
      My eyes fluttered open. A tube was blowing cool oxygen into my nose.
Tubes with needles punctured my veins in my arms and bags of various sizes
and colors drained into me.
      The room was white, pure white. From the ceiling to the floor, even
the bed and sheets under me were pure white. Tan straps held down my arms
and legs to the bed. Two straps across my chest and abdomen constricted my
breathing. A small circular window on the white door framed two sets of
eyes staring at me, their baldheads glistening with perspiration.
      A white intercom box attached high on one of the walls screeched
alive and spoke to me: “Hello, Mr. Woodson. How are we feeling this
morning?”
      “How…How do you know my name?” I asked.
      “Oh, Mr. Woodson we know all about you. Trust us, we know all about
you.” The intercom replied.
      The intercom switched off with a crackle and slight feedback. The two
sets of eyes disappeared from the circular window in the white door.
      I could feel the rush of warm wetness gush from my nose and mouth as
my vision blurred as a chemical suit encased figure stepped to my side. The
figure pushed a vacuum like machine to my face and sucked off the blood as
it poured from me. I felt like a cow hooked up to a milking machine at a
dairy farm.
      As I slipped away from consciousness I gurgled, “What time is it?”

      “I woke up here. With this,” Mr. Woodson pointed at the scar running
along side his head. “And they,” he motioned over his shoulder with his
thumb at a doctor reviewing a patient’s chart, “keep calling me Henry
Jones.”
