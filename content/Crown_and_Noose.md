Crown and Noose


One is what the other promises. The power of the bridle is merely the shackle of another. 


We live in a world with a deficit in the rule of law. We've chipped away at our freedoms with the objective of safety and security. We now deserve neither. 


Madness plagues our leaders. Its infection slowly growing in our societal veins. Flash fires of this insanity rocket across the world in minutes and we feed on it. The outrage satisfied now with a few digital screams and ALL-CAPPED messages. We feed this anger because it tames the void of power we suffer every day. 


What if we are the evil in the world? 
What if everything you know is opposite of what you thought it was? 


God is a migraine. But you function with it regardless. 
You function despite of it. Before you begin to hate me. Before you pass judgment on this sinner's soul. Remember, I believed in God - it - with reasoning before I stopped buying bullshit futures on the stock market of our souls. 


I used to believe there was a reason for all of this. Then I met her. 
I used to believe there was purpose. Then I met chance. 
I used to believe the heavy heads that wear the crowns carried that weight because of some higher purpose, reason, or mission. But there is no guiding hand. There is not divine force. There is only the good that men do despite the actions of others. The pen across the executive order that kept children from the teet of liberty is not heaven sent. It is not divine or holy in purpose. Neither is the ink that granted so many wayward hearts relief with the promise of  the pathway toward freedom. Neither of these are the crown nor the noose. They are both the same. The stoke that bars the heart from beating freedom is the same as the one that cuffs the hands and locks the future in prison cells. 


Even in the most powerful seat known to our American minds, there is captivity. There is no absolute power, not even in the destruction of the chains that bind the money changers and greed-lovers. 


Much like his cousin, the pauper - staring at you through the windshield of your car as you tap the steering wheel listening to the report of the traffic jam you currently reside in. You pray to avoid the eye contact of the hollowed face and mangy haired remainder of society. But he is there. Hse is there. Holding that hand scribbled sign. Misspelled, but doing the one thing that we fear most; begging. 


The light changes and you inch forward toward your objective. Your work. The true salvation of the modern predicament. The thing that enslaves us all, if not the master than the means to serve the master with. 


I speak of money. We are all held to its grasp. Presuming you paid for this. But if you did not transact with something of value for this, I curse you and piss in your mother’s mouth. Yes, because she is your weak spot. She’s all our weak spots because of the western transitive guilt that shames us all for taking form her the thing we needed most. Life. The blood and body came after. Some say life begins at conception. Others not until vitality. Still many others say life does not understand itself until it understands life. BUllshit. Life is what life is. How dare you try to quantify it in any of its forms. You cannot say life begins once the division of cells occurs and ignore the propulsion of life as it hurtles toward the certain death we all face whether how many days, months, or years it falls separated from the womb. 


It is here where our story begins; screaming, bloody, and frightened. 


Like Gene Krupa on his drums the rain pounded at the panes of the broken glass. Tapping and slapping the wetness of the drops against the crisp distilled silica barrier. The rumble of the thunder the neutered drone bass line of and out of sync tune. 


PRESIDENT KILLED. The stuttering billboard atop the lamppost light flashed the words against the rustled blur of the low hanging tree branches. The flash and this color dashing on the wetness from the rain. 


ASSASSIN AT LARGE. The beat of his heart rumbled in his ears. His wound pulsed with pain. The bold letters shouted through the air sounding in the eyes of our suspect. Blood pooled in his boot, trickling in spurts and jets from a pass-through wound in his thigh. The jets of crimson burned like hell through his nerves; his pain receptors screamed along his nervous system to his brain. 


As he leaned against the brick of the wall in the alleyway he wrapped a handkerchief around his thigh with a pen wound in the knot to tighten the tourniquet. He had to get to the drop zone get rid of the weapon and run to another safe house. But running was not in his deal of cards right now. He had his chance to run, but failed, when the bomb went off and he fired the shot in the confusion. It was his fault too. He should have never thrown the grenade and broke from the plan. He was a daft fool. Like Oswald before him, the patsy waiting for the next bullet to silence his tongue. The pistol - a cheap copy of a popular German 9mm - slapped his ribs as his awkward limp forced an awkward gate. The pistol jammed and forced him to throw the backup hand grenade instead. He was shot in return and in the mad rush from the scene he found his thigh ripped through with a shot fired from some other gun - he did not know if it was a police officer’s or another assassin's.


Two more blocks, then a left and into the back door of the chinese laundry off 5th street. His mind tried to encourage him to press forward, limping, bleeding, and exposed. 


A man and woman walking close together under a small umbrella passed by our Assassin. She saw the ad hoc bandage and screamed, the man shushed her and quickly bolted with his hands around her shoulders into the rainy street. A bus slammed into the both of them. Our assassin had just killed two more. Or would these be marked in his murder column. The man did press the screaming bitch into traffic. The assassin was not driving the bus. He was merely the excitement to this scene. 


A police car’s blue and red lights flashed and a siren sounded just down the street, its engine gunned and tries squelched against the rain soaked pavement. 


Our Assassin charged from his corner in the alleyway out toward the street, past the stopped bus with its victims underneath. More screams. More shouts. Commands to stop. Commands to raise his hands. Our Assassin ignored them and limply ran half dragging his wounded leg toward the intersection. 


Shots fired. 


Thunder clapped. 


Lightning flashed. 


Our Assassin felt the bull-like punch of the bullet in his shoulder. Another through his hip. He spin round and fell into the intersection of the street. Police officers charged toward our fallen Assassin. But the southbound express bus found him first. 


Screech of the hot brakes against the wet wheel drums. 


Thump-thump of the shoulders and torso of our Assassin under the hard rubber of the wheels and bouncing up against the twisting drivetrain and metal undercarriage of the bus. 


If you felt pain for our Assassin, do not. He only felt the crush of the first set of wheels; for brief moment the sudden pressure and then blackness. His life was gone before the second double set of rear tires separated his head from his mangled body and crushed his legs separating them from his hips. His job done. His death achieved. His pain over. His mercy delivered. Despite these facts, his story survives; as does many of these tales.


The red and blue flash of the police lights peppered the scene freezing rain drops mid-air as  crowds formed around the carnage. The violence done but the curious plebs still craning their necks to see bloody white rain-soaked sheets under bus wheels. 


From the floor to ceiling window in the Assassin’s apartment overlooking the intersection, Mr. Ruby stood jotting a note onto his small notepad with purple latex gloved hands. He flipped the cover and slipped it into his breast pocket of his sport coat. He scooped up the small 9mm handgun from the bar counter next to him. And dropped it into a plastic evidence bag. Slipping his hands from the purple latex gloves he reached into his pocket and pulled a rectangular phone, slid the cover down and pressed a three button code and the Send button. The gloves dropped into another plastic bag. A handkerchief whipped from his front pocket and he ran it along the edge of the counter, light switch, and doorknob of the apartment. He took an umbrella from the stand by the door and twisted the doorknob with his handkerchief hand. The light from the hallway poured into the room exposing a bloodied carpet the body of another man laying on his back, shot in the face - blood pooling. 


Mr. Ruby took one look back and saw no footprints left on the hardwood floors and grunted his satisfaction. The door swung shut and the lock clicked. Mr. Ruby exited the building with his head down and umbrella opened walking away from the carnage on the street and took the subway entrance and first train away from the area. 


The bag with handgun was never found, but passed harmlessly into the dumpster in the loading dock of the building into the belly of the garbage truck the next morning and ultimately into the bowels of the earth outside of Washington, D.C. with the remainders of yesterday's refuse from thousands of other homes and apartment buildings. 


The newspapers proclaimed, ASSASSIN KILLED IN ACCIDENT RUNNING FROM POLICE. 


Awkward calm descended over the nation's capital the next day, but the death of the President was still fresh on palate of the world.
