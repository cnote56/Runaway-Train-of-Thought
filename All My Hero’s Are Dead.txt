All My Hero’s Are Dead; And My Loved Ones Are Dying

“All my Hero’s are dead; and my loved ones are dying.”

The static over the transmission signal filled the void between her
sentences. She began again.

“I am the last of my people.”

Flashes and the bustle of the drone cameras hovering nearby caught the
silence between moments.

Behind our speaker is a green wall of marble.

Her yellowed skin, a sign of atmosphere poisoning from her time on the
colony and growing up on the moon didn’t help her immune system when she
had to come to Earth.

She could speak through her respirator, but it meant something more for
her to brave the natural air without a mask.

“Breathing this Earth’s air has been bittersweet. Because I know I must
breath this air and share it with you, while I know what you have done
to my people. To my family, To my very life.”

Remqa DeVein stood clad in her miner’s uniform. Still with the badging
and flag of the Asteroid Cosortium - a known unionist group from within
the burgeoning and at times lucrative field of asteroid mining.

Remqa coughed and tried to clear her throat as she began again. -the
earth’s air was heavy with particulates, more so than the refined air
used in her normal - but hardly natural - environment in space.

She is here, in the last driest portions of New York to plead with the
United Worlds And Nations to act and seek a correction for wrongs that
have gone ignored and spurred a growth of injustice that has divided a
solar system.

She began to tell her story in her native tongue - a form of English,
but it was peppered with abbreviations and euphemisms that were more
novelty than communication to the blasied and disconnected rich here on
Earth.

To the poorer class of workers, they understood more closely the story
Remqa began to tell.

“The exploitation of the asteroids was meant to uplift humanity. Instead
it has enslaved the most vulnerable of ourselves and taught our children
another value system. A value system that ignores the human in front of
you and rewards the institution that whips their back to shreds.

“I have survived it. What is this ‘it’ I speak of? It is the very thing
I stand in front of today.” She waved her hand dismissing the green
marble backdrop of the old building she stood in.
