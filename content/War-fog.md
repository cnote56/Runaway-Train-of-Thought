Lord Bellomont and Captain Kidd had a complex and controversial
relationship. Lord Bellomont was the colonial governor of New York,
Massachusetts Bay and New Hampshire in the late 17th century. He was
also a major financial sponsor of Captain Kidd, who was a Scottish
privateer commissioned to hunt down pirates and enemy French ships in
the Indian Ocean. ¹²

However, things did not go as planned for Kidd. He failed to find many
targets, lost much of his crew and faced threats of mutiny. He also
captured a few ships that were either friendly or neutral to England,
which raised suspicions of his loyalty. One of these ships was the
Quedagh Merchant, a valuable vessel hired by Armenian merchants and
captained by an Englishman. Kidd claimed that he had a French pass that
authorized him to seize the ship, but he could not produce it at his
trial. ¹³

When Kidd returned to the American colonies, he tried to persuade Lord
Bellomont of his innocence. Bellomont, however, sent him to England for
trial, and he was found guilty of the murder of one of his crew members,
William Moore, and on five indictments of piracy. He was hanged in 1701.
¹²⁴

Some historians have argued that Kidd was a victim of political intrigue
and that Bellomont betrayed him to protect himself and his associates
from being implicated in Kidd's activities. Others have suggested that
Kidd was indeed a pirate who tried to cover up his crimes with false
claims and excuses. The truth may never be known for sure, but the story
of Bellomont and Kidd remains a fascinating and tragic one. ¹⁴
