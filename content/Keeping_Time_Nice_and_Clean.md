In a reality where time control is as common as hailing a taxi. There arises a need to keep the timeline from countering the established powers that be, the Fatherhood owners and operators of the Time Corporation.
They correct wrong murders and false fortunes. 
They guide the moments that create the cornerstones of our society. 
The laundrymen travel through time cleaning up potential paradoxes and bad outcomes that may affect the control held by the Fatherhood. 
Until one Laundryman cannot complete his mission and a young woman's existence stands to topple the powers that be in this true-crime inspired and time traveling steeped tale. 
Will the timeline survive? 
Will the actions of a few control the future? 
###
“What the hell are you doing?” Spencer Triga was too angry to yell. Besides the lunchtime cafe was far too crowded for an argument not to be noticed. “I want you to tell me why you just ordered a bowl of soup and you are not somewhere more important than right here. He pointed his long fingers like a knife blade down at the Formica tabletop. 
Spencer’s gestures were swift precise with exactly as much energy had to be used to accomplish the meaning of the gesture. 
The creature suffering the verbal assault Spencer was currently spitting was Chance Griffin. Chance was not supposed to be sitting in the booth of the busy diner at lunchtime in mid-town New York on June 16, 2015. He was supposed to be at 721 Fifth Avenue, New York. He was not. This was the most abundantly clear point of Spencer’s reserved but emotionally animated ongoing speech. Chance had muted his ears when the gestures grew tighter and more katana-like. 
You’re not going to ignore me, Chance. Spencer’s mouth was no longer moving. This can go on all god damn day, but you fail to answer my question. 
“I don’t care anymore, Spence.” Chance sighed and sucked his lungs full as he pitched forward on his elbows. “I don’t fucking care any. More.” 
The pause in his delivery was intended to emphasize the intent of the word not necessarily the meaning of the words. 
You don’t get to not care anymore Chance. Now, get up and let's go back.  
“But my soup!” Chance protested just as the bowl of vegetable beef sloshed down from the waitress’ wrist-braced hand. 
“Nothing, I can get for you? Sugar?” 
“The check, thank you.” Spencer did not want to waste another minute on blending in.
She slapped the check on the table and spun away into the busy shuffle of people eating, getting up, and sitting while reading, talking, and burping. 
“We’re going,” Spencer said as Chance plunged and retrieved a spoon into the steamy bowl and slurped it down. 
Aghast Spencer said, “You idiot. That’s hot soup. In June. it’s 75 degrees out and humid. And you order soup.” 
Chance plunged and retrieved another mouth full. SLURP.  
“You fucking moron.” Spencer continued. “Is your programming all fucked up? It has to be because only a dysfunctional idiot would order soup on a god damned summer day.” 
Chance plunged and retrieved another mouth full. SLURP.
“You are eating hot soup. Humans have these things in their mouths called taste buds they are very sensitive to temperature. You're a fucking moron because you’re angry at me and eating you stupid fucking soup like a fucking fool that you can’t tell if your mouth should be blistering and burnt like a real human. But it isn’t you fucking dolt!”
Spencer paused for fear of drawing attention to these two strange men.  
Chance plunged and retrieved another mouth full. SLURP.
“But no, You’re a fucked up little tin man that has a bad gear and we’re gonna go dig it out of your fucking head and then we’ll see if you ever see the front line of another mission so long as I’ve got a god damn pulse in my dick!”
Chance plunged and retrieved another mouth full. SLURP.
“Get the fuck up or I’ll pop us right here right now and it will be messy and fuck up a shit ton of other things. Shit tons of things that we don't have time for. So we’re going to get up. I’ll pay for your soup. You take the back exit and I’ll be three steps behind you.”
Chance stopped eating the soup. The last spoon full drooled out of his mouth back into the bowl. He sat straight up and slid out of the booth and out the back exit exactly as Spencer had foretold. 
Spencer slapped a twenty dollar bill on the check. Two cups of coffee (one untouched) and a bowl of vegetable beef soup. $16.45. He stiffed her on the tip because he did not want the table to memorable. A big tip was more likely to be remembered. Shitty tippers are a dime a dozen. 
Spencer slid out of the booth and straightened his tie as he felt the hair on the back of his neck raise up. Pulse weapons were nearby. But they should not be nearby. They should not even be in this time period.
He raced toward the rear exit and out to the alleyway. 
The greasy plastic smell of burnt exoflesh and synthetic tissue shot through Spencer’s nostrils. The hairs on his neck stiff as a youthful dick. 
His hand plunged into his jacket pocket after a thin black device shaped by a minimalist tv remote. Three buttons along the top. A big center button surrounded by a soft ring and to flippers under that that moved up and down but not back and forth. It vibrated and buzzed as his fingertips unlocked with his biometrics. 
Waving it ahead of himself, Spencer rushed through the alleyway exit off the kitchen of the diner to find Chance clutching his throat and gurgling. Oily red and brown liquid spurted from his wounds. One in his leg just above his knee. Not fatal. One across his hip. Again, not fatal. Spencer scanned the alleyway to see no hostile targets. 
“Three.” Chance gruggled as his eye met Spencer’s. “Ssss. Sssss...Steps.” And the light that was in all living things left Chance. 
Spencer stood over his now dead partner and sucked his teeth. He wanted to kick himself. 
I wasn’t where I was supposed to be. FUCK!
Spencer sighed again and stretched his neck skyward. He pressed his thumb against a long button on the device in his hand and in a flash of circular light Chance and Spencer vanished.

He let his attention get pulled away by the cleavage of a waitress only to look back and see the empty booth. He scrambled to pull a money from his jean pockets and spilled quarters and nickels on the floor. 
Out the back exit Pluck ran. 
The waitress yelled, “Hey! This ain't enough! MERV! He’s runn-”  
Out in the alleyway Pluck bolted through the door to find. An empty alleyway. 
        Pluck digs his feet in to stop and slips on a reddish brown oily liquid where Chance had died. 
“What the fu-” Plucky said feeling the liquid on his hands and his clothes. 
“You! Fucking Runner?! You’re not supposed to stop!” Merv the fry cook with a greasy apron tied across his beer-belly. “Dumb fucking millennials! It’s called DINE and DASH! Run!”
Merv towered over Pluck with a broomstick in his hands. Pluck grimaced for the strike but it didn’t come. He opened an eye to see Merv frozen. Not just holding back but frozen. The hairs on his chest were shaking from the beat of his heart - which had to have been a tremendous jostle. His nostrils were flaring. Frozen. 
Pluck looked up and down the alleyway to see a cat paused mid-stride across a trickle of water. Drops of rainwater from the clogged gutter five stories up hung like perfect spheres of anomaly all in line and timed to the clock of gravity. 
Pluck rolled out from under Merv. The oily muck on his jacket coating his backside and staining his hands. He clamored to his feet and started to run but then saw the motionless and dead look in Merv’s eyes. It was as if the freeze was killing him. A pained look on his face formed. Somehow the sudden bend of time in the continuum must have suddenly been affected by something in that paused and unfortunate moment. Pluck felt it. It was empathy for Merv at the moment that Pluck could have simply run away he looked back to see his aggressor in pain and paused out of empathy. And suddenly Merv unfroze.
“RRRRRuuuuun!” Merv shouted and crashed the wooden broomstick against the oily patch was he had just seen the shitty kid who ran out on the tab. But he wasn’t there. 
Merv looked up the alleyway and his eyes met with Plucks. 
“How did you… fuck it, I don’t care. Fuck! You! Kid!” Merv took the broom and threw it like a javelin at Pluck only to miss his as Pluck retreated away and out of the alleyway at the street.  
“What the fu… YOU BETTER RUN!” Merv shouted and returned to the diner.
