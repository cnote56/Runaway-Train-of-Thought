the Earth. If you picture the Man in the Moon formed by the dark lunar

perspective, visualize the Moon as a globe with the familiar face we

lunar surface.

The valley system runs roughly north-south, arcing across the Moon's
eastern limb in a general region analogous to the far western Pacific
between Japan and Hawaii's longitude on Earth.

As we approach the valley, a base becomes visible in a box canyon with a

airlocks on the lunar surface.

The BRIC Alliance claimed the valley at the mouth of the canyon and the
remainder of the hemisphere, precisely two-thirds of the dark side and
one-third of the light side. The remainder was left to the US-Euro
Federation.

The Alliance is an ambitious coalition formed by the nations of Brazil,
Russia, India, and China. United by their determination to establish a
permanent human presence on the lunar surface, these four countries have
pooled their resources and technological expertise.

While the United States and its allies have squandered opportunities for
crewed lunar exploration, the BRIC nations recognized the strategic
importance of claiming the Moon's vast resources and establishing
off-world habitats. Overcoming political differences, they constructed
efficient multinational programs.

Brazil brought advanced financing prowess and industrial engineering
brilliance. Russia leveraged decades of hard-won space experience and
proven rocket testing facilities. India provided a deep pool of talented
scientists and engineers at a fraction of Western costs. China's vast
industrial might and exceptional manufacturing capacities kept the
venture afloat.

Together, through a collaborative lunar program named “Chandrasangam,”
the BRIC nations have constructed Shanti Nagar - the first permanent
multinational colony on the Moon in Vallis Schroteri. While publicly
promoting the scientific exploration of space, the BRIC alliance sees
this as the vanguard for further human expansion and resource extraction
across the solar system.

The United States and its traditional space partners cling to their
waning influence while dismissing or attempting to undermine the upstart
international lunar colony through espionage and sabotage. American
spies seek to steal technological secrets or, worse, provoke an armed
conflict to reassert dominance over any extraterrestrial resources. The
race to the final frontier has taken an adversarial turn.

Joseph Campbell's Hero's Journey, also known as the monomyth, is a
narrative structure in many different cultures worldwide. It tells the
story of a hero who goes on an adventure, faces challenges, and
ultimately returns home transformed.

The Hero's Journey typically follows these twelve stages:

1. The Call to Adventure: The hero is called to leave their ordinary
world and embark on an adventure.

2. Refusal of the Call: The hero may initially refuse the call, but they
are eventually persuaded to go on the adventure.

3. Meeting with the Mentor: The hero meets a mentor who helps them
prepare for their journey.

4. Crossing the First Threshold: The hero crosses a threshold into the
unknown world.

5. Tests, Allies, and Enemies: The hero faces challenges and makes
allies along the way.

6. Approach to the Inmost Cave: The hero approaches the center of the
unknown world.

7. The Ordeal: The hero faces a major challenge or obstacle.

8. Reward: The hero is rewarded for their efforts.

9. The Road Back: The hero begins the journey back to their ordinary
world.

10. Resurrection: The hero is transformed by their experiences.

11. Return with the Elixir: The hero returns to their ordinary world
with a new understanding or gift.

12. Epilogue: The hero shares their newfound knowledge with others.

The Hero's Journey is a powerful story structure that can be used to
tell a wide variety of stories. It has been used in many different works
of literature, film, and television, including The Odyssey, The Lord of
the Rings, Star Wars, and The Matrix.
