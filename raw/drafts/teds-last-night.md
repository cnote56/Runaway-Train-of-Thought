Ted’s Last Night                             ~340 words
Cole Dowden
Cole.dowden@gmail.com; 817-343-3718

      Ted said he felt all right, but he looked like shit to me. He was
sweating all night even thought it was 45 degrees outside and it had been
about 60 all day long.
      It was Friday and we were headed to our favorite bar. Him and me had
been friends since high school. We had both nursed each other through hell
and back; failed relationship after failed relationship both our college
degrees and his failed marriage, which he was till subject to.
      You know he didn’t look right all night long. Sweating and swilling
down drink after drink trying to cool off something fierce. Think he had
just had a fight when I picked him up. I could hear her shouting at him
about his muddy boots and him screaming back about the cold food.
      That’s where I think the problem was with his strategy. You see,
alcohol never put out any fire I had ever seen before. His speech slurred a
little bit as he turned to me and said, “Buddy, I don’t feel too good.”
      I expected Ted to vomit. This was about that time of the night.
      Then Ted burst into flames.
      Everybody just freaked out; I just sat there watching my friend smile
at me through the flames. He took a sip of beer as his flesh turned black
and leaned back in his chair mumbling something about his favorite dancer
not being at the bar tonight.
      Yeah, Ted’s shoes didn’t burn though. In the dust and ash of his old
bones there were his shoes; spotless. I took those size nine’s home with
me. I’m never one to waste a good pair of shoes.
      For a while there I was wondering what I was to say to his wife but
after she opened the door with a smile and her black beads from Jamaica
hanging from her hair and neck I had a feeling she knew.
      Yeah, I’m going to miss old Ted but he sure had a good pair of shoes.
