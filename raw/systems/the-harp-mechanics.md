# System Mechanic: The Harp (Frequency Stabilization)

The Harp is a planetary-scale stabilization device critical to the maintenance of reality in the BIC simulation. It acts as an anchor for the planet's frequency engines, which project the forces that bind matter and causality.

## Core Components
- **The Axis Gear:** The central structural unit holding the system together.
- **Sister Circles:** Rotating arrays of vases containing oils and water of specific densities, heats, and measures.
- **The Engine:** Fission-based frequency generators that convert physical rotation into stabilizing reality-waves.

## The Principle of "Varying Degrees"
Stabilization is achieved through fluid dynamics. As oils heat or cool, their density changes, shifting the equilibrium of the sister circles. 
- **Fat Oils:** High density, moisture-retaining.
- **Waters:** Low density, reactive.
- **The Dance:** A continuous cycle where density shifts drive the rotation of the main axis, projecting the harmonic frequencies required to maintain the simulation's integrity against the "Tumble" (casing-stress).

## Failure Modes
- **Desynchronization:** When the sister circles lose balance, the frequency engines oscillate, creating jagged rock-and-roll wavelengths (The Tumble).
- **Physical Manifestations:** Lightning storms, atmospheric instability, mass casualties among "frequency-sensitive" species, and structural collapse of planetary elevators.
- **Sabotage/Apocalypse:** Deliberate manipulation of the control program to induce rapid data-field deletion, resulting in the sector "crashing" or "burning" (the "13.8" event).

## Technical Note: Integration with Fibonacci Engine
The Harp's rotation frequency is calibrated to the BIC Fibonacci-stress damage sequence. If the rotation exceeds safe parameters, the system triggers a Tumble event.
