The story of J.C. Sorenson
By Cole Dooling

I don’t remember how I got home. I just remember standing with the door of
my apartment open behind me and my keys in my hand. I walked to the
refrigerator got a beer out and sat down on the couch. I beer tasted flat
and the cable was out; I leaned back on the couch and tried to relax.
      My eyes hurt; I could feel them pulse inside their sockets. Every vein
in them all the way back to the optic nerves. They hurt from the florescent
lights that poured over me all day.
      My hands ached, from all the endless typing of reports—report after
report after report. The tips of my fingers ached and pains shot through
the tendons all the way up my arms.
      My shoulders could not relax. They were caught pinched and tense. The
muscles were not combinations of tissue, blood and electro-chemical pulses;
they were masses cancerous masses of pain that ached every time they moved.
      Tonight seemed different to me as I shuffled from room to room. I was
wearing the same white shirt and black pants I had always worn but my
clothes felt different as if they were not even on me. My shirt just hung
from my shoulders and the sweat, that usually stuck my shirt to the small
of my back, was not there. Today was a hot day and yet I’m not burning up
here in my apartment sans air conditioning. My necktie, the theoretical
noose that I hung myself with every morning since joining the machinery of
the working class, was loosened a little bit and swung like the pendulum of
clock as I leaned forward and tried to rub my aching neck.
      I usually would get a burrito or six from the taco stand on the street
corner of my apartment building for dinner but I do not even remember the
walk home much less anything else after the two o’clock meeting I had today
with my supervisor.
      Downsizing, he called it downsizing—I was fired and not even offered
any severance. After all my time spent working in that dump, I get
“downsized.” I got a little mad—hell, I got a lot mad—I was boiling with
rage.
      I don’t remember saying goodbye to anyone—not like they would have
cared.
      Perhaps Lacy, she was the receptionist. She was a young one, innocent
till you talked to her, but I liked her style. I liked they way she could
spit venom one minute and them cover you with honey the next. She was not
too easy on the eyes, but she could still get the job done. I once tried to
ask her out. She liked the local basketball team and I liked to drink so I
offered to watch the game with her at a bar that I considered my forwarding
address. She wasn’t interested in the outing she said she had to take her
sister to the airport. I was not going to let that ruin my plans with beer.
After the rejection happened relations were strained. I could tell she was
not interested in me, but I showed my hand when I made the first move.
Therefore all hope was lost and my plane was shot down just after take
off—and I suspected sabotage.
      Henry was the only guy in the office with whom I would speak. He was a
handsome guy who always had a smile for you. We never hung out outside the
office but seemed cordial enough for a meaningless friendship.
      I stopped talking to him when I saw the both of them at a bar drinking
and flirting on  the same night Lacy had refused me. I stood outside in the
rain and contemplated the actions I could take.
      Last Friday, she was drinking cherry vodka sours—the classic girl
drink. He was swilling back Heineken—a good choice if you like the taste of
ass and pig feces in liquid form. They were enjoying themselves—laughing,
smiling and flirting. In my mind, I walked in there and confronted the two
of them.
      In my mind, I knocked out Henry with one punch to the jaw.
      In my mind, I made Lacy cry with a few choice words about her moral
objectivity and sexual history. But, my body stood in the rain fuming
trying to let the rain drown out the white-hot anger that was pulsing
through me. I did not go into the bar and confront them. I turned to my
right and walked home.
      All that was last Friday, and today was Monday. The cold water
sputtered out of the tap in the bathroom and pooled in my hands. The water
bubbled and gurgled over the rim of my cupped hands. My face felt rough
even with the water washing over my face and head. My hair felt greasy and
dirty from the heat and actions of the day.
      My mind’s eye blurred backwards in time to this morning and I found
myself lying in my bed. I awoke to the shrill electronic pulse of my alarm
clock. My shirt was hanging on the doorknob to my bathroom. It was wrinkled
but wearable for my standards and I had no time to iron it. The coffee in
my coffee maker was bitter but the part of my complete breakfast. I was
late to work this morning, but nobody cared that I was running late. More
coffee is always a positive point about my employment. The coffee is free,
not good, but free and with enough sugar and cream tolerable.
      My supervisor called me into a meeting with him and dropped the bomb
on me soon after a stale ham sandwich that I got from the coffee shop on
the first floor.
      He said it was downsizing and a good referral would be available for
me. The he had the gall to ask if everything was all right when he saw that
my head was being cupped in my hands.
      “Nothing,” I said, “Nothing is wrong. Why should anything be wrong?
Should there be anything else to say? I’m fired or ‘downsized’ whichever
spin makes you feel better so you can go home tonight fuck that fat bitch
you call a wife and eat a big steak dinner! Nothing is wrong with me I’m
just fucking fired that’s all!”
      “Sorenson! That is not necessary,” the fat pig uttered in fear.
      I could see the fear welling in his eyes when he saw me stand up and
being to pace from right to left in front of his desk. My eyes and his
locked and there was silence while I fumed.
      “I am trying to be professional about this. Your…” he meekly spoke.
      I interrupted, “I am sick of all this shit! Go fuck your self and tell
you wife I said hi!”
      He turned his head to grab the phone I presume to call security and
that’s when it hit him, the notebook computer. It was on his desk and now
in my hands and repeatedly pounding against the back of his skull. I
watched myself hit him I can’t say I enjoyed it but from the look in my
eyes as I witnessed I can say it must of felt good.
      I dropped the notebook computer beside the body, adjusted my tie
pushed my hair back from my face and strolled out of the office. I calmly
walked to Henry’s office. I stopped at Henry’s office door and popped my
head around the corner, only to pay my respects.
      “Hey Henry, old buddy, old pal. You know I think you should ask that
Lacy girl out for a drink. I think she might be interested in you,” I spoke
with a sinister look in my eye.
      Henry slowly replied, “Uh…sure Jay, thanks for the tip.”
      I snapped my fingers and tapped my forehead, “Oh yeah that’s right you
already did you back stabbing pig! Let me ask you one thing. Last Friday
after the beer and the vodka took effect did you go home and fuck the
living hell out of each other? Did you make her call your 35-year-old ass
Daddy? Oh that’s right your married! Did the wife mind that tight little 23-
year-old ass bouncing up and down beside her as she tried to get some sleep
or did you just fuck at her place perhaps you didn’t even wait to go home?
Perhaps you two just fucked in the alleyway like dogs? Like filthy fucking
rotten dogs!”
      Henry sat in his vinyl chair stunned, staring at me and frozen by my
vitriolic speech.
      I plopped down in the chair in front of his desk, kicked my feet up
onto his desk, and waited for him to respond.
      “What did you just say to me? How did you? Where did you? When…” he
stuttered.
      “Let me finish! It was raining I was walking home. I saw the both of
you laughing, drinking and flirting down at McDowns Pub. She was wearing a
blue dress. You had on a red tie a white shirt and black pants—sans wedding
ring might I add. She was drinking vodka sours—cherry it looked like to me.
You were swilling down Heinekens. I was watching from the window and being
soaked to the bone by the rain,” I leaned forward with my knuckles pressed
on the surface of his desk, “Hey I hear their trimming the fat around here
and doing some cost cutting. I should know I’m some of it!” I shot up like
a rocket and paced like a bull in front of his desk.
      “Listen Jay, I’m sorry but Lacy and I are only friends what happened
on Friday night was just a mistake. I was drunk and she was drunk. We have
already talked about it. I didn’t know you were interested in her. I,” he
tried to plead with me.
      “Stop,” I held my hand up palm facing him, “Henry this is a
predicament we are in isn’t it? I just wanted to wish the two of you good
luck. Go! Go have fun! Fuck like rabbits!”
      Henry’s face was awash with a pleading look.
      “Fuck!” I said.
      “Jay,” Henry started.
      “Fuck!” I interrupted.
      “Listen man!” Henry’s nervous voice shook with fear.
      “Fuck!… Fuck!… FUCK! FUCK! FUCK!” My voice seethed with anger, “Too
bad I think that might be a little different now than from the last time
don’t you?”
      I leaned over his desk with a grin and looked him in his eye and
lunged for a fire extinguisher fastened to the wall.
      He didn’t have time to respond. I had grabbed a fire extinguisher from
off the wall and lunged at him. The extinguisher meet the side of his head
twice before it slipped from my hands and crashed to the floor. His head
was half way through the computer monitor and the muscles in his arms were
twitching from the motor neurons that were still firing in his brain.
      I strolled out of Henry’s office to see shocked and horrified faces on
every one of my former office workers. All their eyes trained on me; all of
them scared to death of what I might do next.
      Then I saw Lacy peeking around the corner from her desk in the
elevator lobby.
      “Lacy! Hey there! How you doing, sweetie? Just talked to Henry,” I
thumbed in the direction of Henry’s office my hands were covered in blood,
“He said that you were one hell of a lay! And all he had to do was buy you
a couple of drinks! So what do you say, want to fuck me for a promotion! Oh
wait that’s right I’ve been downsized and can’t get you any further up the
ladder. Oh Doh! That’s right you weren’t interested in me anyways. You’d
rather fuck a married man! Is it fun? Ruining a family? Hey wait where you
running off too?”
      I began to chase after her but paused at the bell of the elevator and
looked into the eyes of the people on it. They saw me splattered with
blood.
      “I’m sorry we’re having a few technical difficulties right now if you
would like to wait here in the lobby somebody should be with you in a
moment,” I said with a strange calmness.
      My black tie swung like a pendulum half soaked with blood and my chest
heaved with strained effort. My shoulders ached and left arm felt numb.
      I heard some one say “freeze” and as I swung round to see whom spoke I
saw a security officer aiming a gun at me. Suddenly a bright flash engulfed
my vision. I didn’t remember any of this till I pulled my hand back, from
running my fingers through my matted hair, to see blood on them. I touched
the right side of my head just above my ear as a finger slipped into a
hole. I touched my face and found an entry wound just above my left eye.
      My head turned to see and empty apartment and the light streaming into
through the window. I stood there in my silence as I watched the sun stream
in and out of the windows in a strange fast forward action and witness
streaks of human forms revolving around my specter—frozen in space and time
like a column in a temple. I was clutching the impact of my actions and
witnessing the effect. I could not hear anything. I could not speak. All I
could do was feel. Feel the hole in the back of my head, the ache of my
shoulders and the numbness of my hands.
