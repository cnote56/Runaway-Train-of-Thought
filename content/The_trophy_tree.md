The Trophy Tree

By Cole Dowden

I am not human any more. I only resemble the human in biological
standards. My mind is firing on a different level than other humans. I
know because I know. The denial of truth, which I perceive, is a falsity
and must be ignored. This is my experience as a creature—an animal,
because that is all I can assure that I am. I am not human anymore, I
could have been once before, but now I am certain that I am not human
anymore. I am not above humanity nor below it nor on the same level. I
cannot afford the price of humanity’s association in this reality.

I have been struggling in this jungle. Dodging the snakes and poisons
that attack my person without warning. I have become cold, almost alien
to even myself. I act like an animal would act—wild, instinctual,
focused and primitive—because that is the way the strong have survived
and will survive in this reality.

Logic is engraved in the blood—to give me quick reactions with little
debate—right is right and wrong is wrong when considered by the judgment
and logic of my self.

The actions performed at me—actions in offence to me—are illegal and
acts of personal war.

I have acted instinctually and done what my best interest dictates to
me. My logic cannot be questioned and therefore my actions and logic are
one.

I have the skulls of my enemies on the braches of a tree I planted
before I could remember, but I know I planted this tree with the hands
that are mine. This is my trophy tree. I sleep under its braches so my
unconscious can see what my conscious has to battle everyday. My dreams
do not frighten themselves when they have to live under those skulls.
And in return, they have thanked me for my troubles in protecting them
with these skulls, because without these skulls—the evidence of my
physical battles and sacrifice my dreams would have no sanctuary and
protection when they play.

I have to add a new skull when there are frights delivered on my dreams
or myself. The latest skull was a despicable creature. It would attack
my water supply where I drink and bathe and without delay attempt to
overrun my trophy tree and steal a trophy from my tree. I caught him in
my tree reaching for the biggest one on the highest branch. I caught him
by the tail and pulled him down. He fought to get away but I held on to
him too tightly for him to escape.

Mercy is a fool’s dream—an excuse to do the opposite of what we must
do—the ignorance of reality. I killed that creature and added his skull
to the tree. I am protected now from another animal attacking my dreams,
stealing a skull, or creating a skull from me. I do what needs to be
done when it needs to be done. The animal that attempted to steal my
trophy is dead. His skull is now hanging just below his object of
attraction. I do not pity him. I respect him as all the rest of my
trophies. In fact, I speak with them although they do not always speak
kindly in response or at all some times; they still remain in my trophy
tree. I am not the predator. I do not do this for thrill, food,
accomplishment, or to nullify my boredom. My actions and the trophies I
collect are all necessary.

Life feeds on life.

I understand that statement. I understand that for my life to continue
in this wild jungle I must kill the ones that would kill me. My trophy
tree is a testament to my actions and my existence.

Battles

There came along my first trophy. I was young, immature and impetuous.
He sat at the base of my tree. I found him resting there waiting for me.
He spoke softly and questioned me about my tree. After all his
complementing he finally acted on his desires for my tree. The battle
was fierce, but now as I stare into the empty sockets of his skull I
thank him for the lesson he taught me. The lesson being always guard
what you hold most sacred and never let another creature call what is
yours his.

The violence of my nature has grown over the years simply due to the
continual bombardment of infidels that venture into my realm. Remember,
I am protecting my dreams—my unconscious possibilities and their future
with my violence. The trophy tree is their insurance to stand sentry
over the horrific nightmares that could happen if I refuse action. Every
day is not filled with battle after battle and blood upon blood. There
have been years gone without assortment delivered upon my unconscious
dreams many times I have even guarded lightly on my days and spent them
happily lounging under my tree.

Even sharing my sanctuary on many occasions with creatures that
respected my trophies without wishing to take them for their own. So
there is balance in my warrior poetry. There is respect among the
like-minded beasts that tread on their journeys through their lives to
plant their own trophy trees.

I have conversed with them. I have dialogued about their own trophy
trees where their dreams mingle and play. I ask them about the
protection for their dreams and find we are brothers in that vein. So I
am not alone in my actions there are others like me endeavoring to
protect their dreams.

Some of my dreams have wondered why I fight for the trophies, which
adorn the braches of my tree. They ask me about my reasons and my
explanation is never rational to them or acceptable in their minds. We
have debated, ad nausem, and sifted through the minutia of the argument.
They have earned the right to query my actions in the addition of skulls
to the trophy tree. My actions for the protection of my unconscious
dreams allow them to protest my actions, but without my actions in
adding skulls there would be no trophies for my unconscious dreams to
protest or be protected by.

Some dreams grow strong and transverse over to my conscious state. They
add and melt with myself; the one writing these words and form a newer
person. The inherit person is now wiser from absorbing the newer states
of unconsciousness so there is progress in my existence if not to
stabilize my self but also to further the reach of the braches of my
trophy tree.

The absorption of newer unconscious dreams is key to the growth of my
conscious self; I have realized this and accepted the subtle change,
which is inherited with this growth. [My dreams although unconscious
does not lose any touch with the reality or conscious world because they
are let loose when I sleep and dream them.]

The transverse from unconscious to conscious simply implies a gaining of
realities power and the receipt of reality.

The receipt of reality

This is early in the unconscious dreams conception. The unconscious
dream must realize that there are consequences in its action not like in
its nighttime excursions.

The unconscious dreams fruition and transcendence is a process by which
the overall maturity of me is concerned. When the new dream is
processing itself into my consciousness from the unconsciousness. This
is maturing process is a difficult one for both the dream and my
conscious self, but when the acclamation is evident. The receipt of
reality is a process by which the self, a composition of both the dream
(unconscious) and the reality (conscious), reinvigorates itself by a
maturing education process. Logic is accepted by the newborn dream like
the heat of a bath.

Subsistence

Subsistence is the existence in and about the improvement by means that
are available to me with digression in my logic. My existence is
evidence of my improvement by my means all subjugated by the violence
that I deploy with creation of my trophy tree.

Everything in my existence is contingent upon my trophy tree and the
intimidation it delivers to the one that would be hostile to my dreams,
my trophy tree or me.

I say again, I am not human, anymore. I cannot afford the tax that
humanity would impose on the security of my trophy tree and my precious
dreams. Therefore all my actions in the protection of my protesting
dreams are warranted for the protection of their protests. Also, if they
protest then I know they are dreaming. Their recipt of reality is key to
both their development and my growth as a creature. This process is a
reality aspect even when they cross over the line from unconscious to
conscious. The gravity of realization forces them to accept the sun
[when it rises], the night [when it comes], changes in their reality and
those forces on the means of subsistence. This is evidence in favor of
my trophy tree.
