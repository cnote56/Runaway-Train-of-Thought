Old Verbs And The Gods That Remember Them

Across the blackness space, a mass of rock, metal, and dust hurled
toward a small yellow star cast far off any reasonable course. Normally
a random chuck of rock has nothing of import, however, this rock was
different. Instead of oblong angles and fjords of jagged rock spiking
from its surface it was smoothed. Almost glassy, but still appearing to
be some collection aggregate materials sandwiched and mixed together to
form this elongated and strange intergalactic trespasser.

To the strange cadre of asteroid and comet miners, this one was a
rainmaker. The scans that could be verified indicated there was an
incredible amount of materials and resources to sap from this hulk.
There was just one incredible caveat. This traveling gold, platinum,
iron, nickel, other ferrite metals, and miscellaneous organic composites
was almost impossible to catch.

Its trajectory was simple enough to map. It followed a degrading path
across the Andromeda systems but at a velocity that stunned all but the
bravest of any of the asteroid and comet miners. Just a few thousand
charged revolutions per nano-second under light speed; this flying ghost
stagecoach of a payday was nearly impossible to catch, much less rob its
treasures.

Asteroid and Comet mining takes a nimble pilot to find the right angle
to land in just the right spot to derive the ores and make the whole
trip profitable. This thing while a tantalizing payday casting itself
across the known universe couldn’t be tamed because no ship or crew ever
dared to try and catch her. Until now.

The Gorgo had seen better days. Those were years ago. Once a sleek
military research and surveying ship it had engaged in missions to
catalog and record all that it contacted while in service.

The Gorgo. It was fresh out of space dock and barely stretching its legs
with a short mission to test its engines when suddenly the space dock
erupted in a series of explosions tearing its metallic skin apart. Then
the inhabited planet below where most of the Gorgo’s crew had just spent
their last real-gravity hours a few moments ago. Suddenly their
homeworld became their former homeworld.

The cause of the destruction, a simple chemical reaction. The oxygen the
atmosphere ignited into a full airborne event and incinerated the whole
planet in a few moments breaths. Then one sudden day a flash of light
took everything away. Left with the Gorgo the only home its crew knew.

“We hit metal, Captain.”

“Excellent, the ore is close to the surface.” The Captian began counting
his profits in his head.

“No, Captain. We didn’t hit an ore vein.”

A video image appeared on the main display of the bridge showing
straight lines and what appeared to be writing of some alien tongue.

“We hit metal.”
