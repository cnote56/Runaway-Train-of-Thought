‘Like and Subscribe’

By Cole Dowden, cole.dowden@gmail.com;

~1600 words

An alarm hollered through the metal corridors of The Pawnbroker. A metal
hulk of a ship currently blazing a trail across a less desirable corner
of space with its engines burning a maddeningly fast but efficient and
manageable pace. Its course, a long slog through the densest and
most-remote backwoods part of the known universe. Why any Interstellar
Captain ever pivots his hull toward the muddy mess of this part of
creation is either revered or immediately laughed down eagerly in one of
three widely known intergalactic languages in any spaceport this side of
the division.

The first and most worrisome Prikinktpises. Prik for short. When
something speaks to you in Prik, they are not usually being kind, and
are likely to stab you in the back. The other Huntonnnease. Hun for
short. This one means they are just trying to get something from you.

The last and lowest tongue has been a part of all our lives since the
first telescreen became the babysiustter to all our offspring as the
struggle to survive took root in all our societies. They call the
language "Terrain." Because it comes from a little speakerbox of a
planet that is so loud, we don't have to amplify the signal. It just
rambles on broadcasting in all its crazy dialects. Its inhabitants,
quite insane. They chirp and snap at each other, but still, nothing gets
done. Nothing gets improved. It is like watching a Ganzoul try and pry
off an HWnanese while its Umpfrakers are disengaged. Now, I know that's
not a widely accepted way of saying it, but I was raised in a different
time and part of the universe, and we had to deal with different things
back then.

The Terrianian language is so coarse and varied. But pervasive like an
infection, it spread across the universe. Those damned words attacked
communications, ripped them apart, and wrestled them back together into
a form that the population took whole cloth and their minds changed.

Before any of us could begin to understand the war waging in our words,
the turnover was quick and sudden. It was the new normal. We had to
accept this was the way now, and we used these words. The Language War
lasted a few days once that damned language found its root in the
communications system. Like a virus on a defenseless population, it took
over, and now everyone throws its useless words out of their mouths.
Pity because it took our languages - our most basic currency between the
ones we loved - and rutted it about the town into a whore's tongue that
no one respects.

And the Territorians. Don't get me started on them. Oh, I'm not afraid
of insulting one of them. I'll do it to its face. Get one on a video
screen right now. It's easy. But the damned ignorant thing would thing I
was trying to ask it to take me to its leader. What the fucking hell
does that trash mean? What tin the fuck-hell indeed. Such base-animals
these Territorians. Ignorant. Simply pitiful and ignorant, really.

Now, don't get me started about trying to defend the damned beasts. They
are likely very oblivious to any of these criticisms because they keep
making these ridiculous parodies of what life is like in the universe.
They are way off base. Like there's one where this weird group of
strangers has to work together to fight off some guy that looks a lot
like a particular MandaGrungian that is trying to run for Priumperant,
and it is just not helping the fringe voters. I mean, they go after his
stubby fingers, and his chin is not that big. Everything coming from
that planet is so off-balance and wildly self-ingenuous that it frankly
borders on such a feat of self-feliciano that often younger viewers are
not allowed to consume.

Aside from getting laughed down by a cadre of stereotypical Interstellar
Captains in the LIQI-BEVs on any starport this side of the division, the
only reason to go digging in the muck and mud of this universe is if you
think you see something worth getting dirty. Here, it was a reading from
a glimmer of a radio scan that fed into the navigation computer of this
ship, The Pawnbroker, that sent it into the pit of this section of the
known universe. Here, the glimmer was just a reflection of old galactic
superstructures floating around a husk of a cinder that could have only
been a planet once long ago. Don't tell any of this to a Territorian.
Those ignorant slobs with try and shout you down in their absurd
language. A smart one will try to throw in a random word - poorly
conjugated, of course - while they try to respond verbally, but it is so
sad when they flutter and squawk. They get unusually fussy about what
was and was not their fault on their homeworld. But I'm not here to pick
a fight about people's sensitivities.

The Pawnbroker's Captain didn't direct the ship into this course. When
possible, any good Captain will avoid going near this area mainly
because it is so expensive to get out of there; it just burns too much
fuel. But the damned Navigation Streams fire off for profit and damned
all other considerations. They never consider the real cost of flying
through awful, dangerous, and poisonous areas of space. But no, the
computer sends the ships where they send them; the next closest and most
profitable - above all other considerations - salvage signal. It's up to
the navigators to shave a few points off the running total and try to
get us a little more profit at the end of the next quarter. Profits. It
always comes down to profits. The fundamental question we all ask
ourselves, "can we make something valuable out of the shit between us?
And who will buy it?"

Welcome to The Pawnbroker, a salvage ship currently headed out of the
milky-way sector and toward a nebula where they received some lucrative
readings to their radio telescope frequencies.

"We're looking at a lot of Quintrillions on that rock there...Wait..why
is that damn bell going off? Where the hell is that boy?"

Henrick scoffed as he went back to his navigation terminal and refined
the degrees plotting that would save much-needed fuel while also
ensuring a good return on the cost of getting to the goldmine they just
found floating in space.

DaLong stomped his mag-boots across the metal grating through the tunnel
system toward the Loadmaster's quadrant. He would be without any legal
authority once he crossed over the airlock into the cargo and salvage
holds. That was the realm of the Loadmaster's domain. They had the final
say on everything having to do with the salvaged parts and anything
valuable on 90 percent of the ship. The Captain gets the helm and
bridge. That's about it.

Profitium Uberatum Alles, under the bold lettering THE PAWNBROKER on the
hull and etched with lasers and painted with the carbon scoring of many
a tariff war. That was the motto on the side of the damned ship that
carried them. And that was the motto inside as well, between the air of
its guts and bowels.

"Kresart!" DeLong bellowed at the intercom as he passed bulkhead after
bulkhead toward the cargo hold. "What are you doing down here that we've
got this damn thing ringing?! Hendriks and I are up there trying to keep
this quarter from going red, and you're down here, jerking it off while
the damn cargo hold goes to shit and blow our eardrums out with the
alarms!"

A wall of monitors showing the various angles and stacks of salvaged
metals and hordes of riches in the cargo holds on them. Some lights were
flashing on them. Others calm and empty. The high backed chair jostled
and jerked. DeLong charged ahead; suddenly worried Kresart was suffering
from a medical condition; That could be expensive and would affect the
profit margin, he thought. He whipped the chair around to see Kresarts
green face twisted in that awful sick form it turns when he's listening
to Terrorian entertainment. And then his mouth gaped open and hurled a
thundering explosion of fetid-air and jellied-saliva in a blast of
pent-up laughter. Prikkeasians only laughed once a day, and Kresart just
did. A clean-bot would be along to wipe up the mess.

DeLong wiped his face and spat his lips clean. Reaching across Kresart,
DeLong tapped a switch and turned the alarm off. He cast his eyes back
at Kresart. Who's shame was slowly eroded back into his usual state of
smugness. The warning meant nothing; it was a slight air pressure leak
in a hold. Pressure checked and now sealed. The switch just needed
turning off. Kresart was too wrapped up in his hand-screen and some
ridiculous transmission from some terrible Terrorian recording half
garbled by their primitive tongues.

"Oh come' on, DeLong," Kresart pleaded as DeLong exited the cargo-hold
control room. "It was a great punchline. I held it as long as I could
this time! You would've laughed too! It was a perfect punchline!!!"

DeLong was out of the room and headed back toward the bridge, where he
belonged.

Kresart slumped back into his highback control chair.

"I don't care what you say, man. It was a great punchline," Kresart
slapped his hand against the screen to start the video over again.
"Worth it." He spoke about the cleaning costs that were currently
running a tab as the ship's clean-bots rumbled and rolled to and fro
under his tentacles.

"'Like and Subscribe,'" Kresart squeaked and spat with a little of his
bile landing on the screen with his tentacles cradling the screen as he
quoted the chortle causing reaction. "Great punchline, 'Like and
Subscribe.' HA!"
