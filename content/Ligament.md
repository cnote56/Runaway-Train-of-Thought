---
title: Ligament
---

You never get used to the sound.

The pop.

The release.

The relax.

The absence of resistance.

It happens right when the one that gives away right before the bone
does.

Everyone fears a broken bone. But they never think about the ties that
bind.

Bits of elastic coil connect every moving bit of ourselves.

You don't move without them,even if your bones remain intact.

I learned this at a young age. You learn a lot when you are fighting off
older brothers and neighborhood bullies.

I learned that my joints had that extra give that others didn't. I never
learned to throw a punch, didn’t have too. I just learned how to get
them to give up.

You never forget the sound. The give-up and give-in sound. Some hear it
as a pop. SOme say they can feel the tear. Others don’t hear anything,
they just can’t use the limb anymore...either permanent or for a little
while.

I hear it so often in my line of work that honestly, I try not think
about it. But I admit, it is satisfying.

They give up so quickly afterwards. No fight left. Still life but no
fight. But I count them dead, because there is no fight without life.

My name is Billy. I like to fight. I don't lose.

They call me, “ Pension,” because guys tend to retire after matching up
with me.

I’ve had nights where I fight four times in a row. I can because I don’t
throw and I don’t jump around. I just get there and get ahold of them.
If that happens; fight’s over.

But tonight, I’ve got only one fight. And he’s got a lot of me in him.

He grapples and is as strong python. I should know, I taught him.

His mother was a fighter. She hated the fights. Didn’t hate the money I
came home with, but she hated the blood and bruises.

She left what seems like a long time ago. So long I couldn’t remember
her face, until I saw his eyes.

He favored her features. Strong chin, almond eyes. His nose has been
broken a number of times - he is a fighter. But here’s was broken a few
times too. I must admit to at least one being by my hands.

I didn’t punch her, she punched me. I locked her up and she struggled
and passed out. I released out of instinct and she crumpled to the floor
banging her face on the island counter in the kitchen.

At least that’s what the survielance footage showed. I did not remember
the evening.

Those days blurred together. The training and fights never ended and
didn’t change. My opponents were flagstones along my path. She wasn’t
supposed to be one of them. She should have been my partner, the one I
looked to in the audience that helps me conjure the strenght to finsh
the fight.

She left me after the broken nose. She had every might to. And she did
the right thing. I was a monster back then. Still am in many ways.

I’ve never remembered that night, but I also can’t forget it. It’s there
when I wake up, alone. It’s there when I come home, alone. It’s there
every time I want to share something with someone, alone.

I’ve prepped for this fight like all the rest of them. I know his
weaknesses. But I can’t win this fight. And I can’t survive another
fight.

The doctors told me very plainly. I’ve worn out the valves in my heart.
Paper thin and barely holding on. I shouldn’t fight again. I shouldn’t
even jog hard. But I’ve got one more fight.

“Ladies and gentlemen, tonight we have the old lion versus the young
lion! Two undefeated brawlers.”

I hope I die from this fight. I don’t want to win against him, not this
way. Killing me might be the best for both of our careers. A merciful
ending - two birds; one stone.
